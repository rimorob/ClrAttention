# Premise review and ideation: why the walk loses on complexes, what is left to try, and which comparator can actually beat us

*12 November 2026. Ideation memo (Claude Fable 5.1) for the PI. Nothing was run, nothing was edited, no truth was opened, no job was touched. Every number quoted below is read from a repo document named next to it. Citations marked **[verify]** were not checked against a primary source in this session; the rest are either repo-cited or standard references I am confident of. Not a preregistration; not for the paper.*

**One-paragraph summary.** The repo has already closed every "normalise differently" route: width (keff 3–40, global temperature, local-FDR keff, multi-head), cap, Sinkhorn and column forms, coarse-graining and residuals, robust row scales, rank/lift/π readouts, and the log-domain CLR(log Pᵗ) readout (works in-sample on K562, not confirmed on an underpowered E. coli complex truth). The CORUM loss is not a harder latent-variable problem; it is a **different estimand**: the walk estimates *shares of a budget*, which are 1/(n−1) inside an exchangeable block by a theorem (`asymmetry_expert.md` §3.1), while co-membership truth rewards an *n-invariant* "same latent" statistic, which CLR's z-score approximates for free. What has **not** been tried, and is cheap, is (1) estimating the n-invariant quantity directly (consensus co-membership over a log-resolution sweep of the attention walk), (2) detecting partner-less genes with an *external* replicate compendium instead of a half-split (the only remaining lever by the repo's own analysis), (3) per-hub-decile depth on a log grid chosen with the existing lab-split criterion (never tested as a method; I checked), (4) a topology-gated hybrid readout on a common z scale, and (5) a self-supervised *learned* attention trained on our own compendium, which is both an idea and the comparator that can falsify the "estimated, not learned" thesis in a way scPRINT could not. For (b) I recommend a prior-vs-inference incremental-value test against scGPT/Geneformer gene embeddings on K562 with two untouched truths, with scPRINT-2 and the self-supervised attention as backups.

## 1. Diagnosis in latent-variable terms

Write x_i = Σ_f b_if F_f + e_i. A **complex** is one latent F measured n times with near-equal loadings (exchangeable copies); a **regulon** is a latent (TF activity) with heterogeneous, weak loadings on m targets plus a hub whose own mRNA is a noisy proxy; regulons overlap. Co-membership truth asks, for a pair, "do i and j share a latent?", a quantity that is **invariant to n**: adding more copies of the ribosome does not make RPL3–RPL4 less of a pair. CLR's row z-score is approximately n-invariant (each copy is judged against ~G non-members; the repo measures +0.09 z per e-fold of complex size, `corum_analysis.md` §3.3). The softmax turns strength into share (rows sum to 1 at perplexity 10, erasing the difference between r = 0.36 and r = 0.81, `asymmetry_expert.md` §3.1), and the walk, inside a well-connected clique, converges to the clique's quasi-stationary distribution: every within-block pair gets m/(n−1), a theorem for any row-, column- or doubly-stochastic normalisation. So the walk estimates **P(a random t-step walk from i ends at j)**, which inside a block is a *share of n*, not **P(i and j load on the same latent)**. The pooled metric and the per-complex AP compare across rows, where ribosome pairs at ~0.009 lose to partner-less genes' junk pairs at ~0.1 and to one-sided satellites (INTS8 0.84 on 60S). Regulons survive because their pairs sit near the MI noise floor, where the walk's *averaging over m noisy copies of the same latent* (variance reduction via the hub) lifts them above the background of other weak pairs; the 1/m division costs less than the denoising gains, and the gain is concentrated in regulons ≥ 100 genes (`paper_plan.md` §5 macro). Small complexes lose by the opposite mechanism, leakage forced by the perplexity floor (in-clique mass 0.23–0.26 at t = 9 for n = 4). **Evidence:** 88% of RP mass stays in the complex at t = 9 and per-partner mass is 0.88/77–95 (dilution, not leakage); row-local AP recovers (0.30 → 0.47) while per-complex AP collapses (0.19 vs CLR 0.66): a calibration signature; the rank readout reverses the ordering (K10-rank 5.13× vs CLR-rank 3.36×), so CLR's lead is cross-row scale; a3r, the first n-invariant readout, rescues K562 in-sample and points the right way in E. coli at 5× the minimum effect but with SE 0.018; the oracle "true width" does not fix an 80-gene clique (×1.04). **Falsifiers:** (i) if an estimator that is provably n-invariant inside exchangeable blocks (idea 1) still loses to CLR on ribosome-dominated CORUM under the same pooled metric, the estimand story is wrong and the loss sits in the MI/softmax input; (ii) if the gain of any n-invariant readout does *not* scale with within-complex tightness (median within-complex CLR z, a label-free stratifier of CORUM), the "copies of one latent" picture is wrong; (iii) if partner-less genes silenced by an external-replicate gate (idea 2) do not lift small complexes on K562 but do on synthetic data, the junk-row lever is a generator artefact.

## 2. Ranked improvement ideas (eight, best first)

Compute references used for the estimates: `corum_analysis.md` scored ~20 readouts with a 100-replicate gene jackknife on K562 (5,671 genes) in 42 min; `robust_z` did five variants with a depth stage in 57 min (M3D) and 4 h 38 min (K562); a dense 5,671² product takes seconds on 8 threads; Leiden on a 5,671-node, ~280k-edge graph takes about a second.

### 1. Consensus co-membership over a log-resolution sweep of the attention walk (the n-invariant estimand)

- **Mechanism.** Estimate P(i and j share a latent) directly as the frequency with which i and j are co-clustered when the attention graph W = (A + Aᵀ)/2 (or Pᵗ at the headline t) is partitioned at resolutions γ on a log grid {2⁻⁶ … 2⁶}, across lab halves (M3D) or profile halves (K562) and seeds. Score_ij = Σ_γ w_γ · freq_γ(i ~ j), with w_γ ∝ γ (fine co-clustering counts more) or uniform. Inside an exchangeable clique every pair is co-clustered at every resolution until the clique itself splits, independent of n; a satellite (INTS8) joins the 60S only at coarse γ; a loose regulon co-clusters only at coarse γ, which ranks it below complexes but above random pairs; a junk pair co-clusters only at the finest γ *and* only in the half that produced it, so the half-consensus suppresses it with no threshold and no row gate. This is Markov stability with Leiden as the optimiser (Delvenne, Yaliraki & Barahona 2010; Lambiotte, Delvenne & Barahona 2014), and it is the overlapping fine-to-coarse "variación" the PI wanted, delivered as a pair score rather than as a module list.
- **Label-free tuning.** Only w_γ and the γ grid. Choose w_γ ∝ the cross-half reproducibility of the partition at γ (variation of information between halves, against a rewired-operator null), so unstable resolutions get no weight. No truth.
- **Cheapest falsifiable test (exploratory, K562 in-sample, ~1 h).** 13 resolutions × 6 halves × 2 directions × 5 seeds ≈ 800 Leiden runs on the K10 operator (minutes) plus the standard 100-replicate paired jackknife against CLR and K10 on CORUM raw/merged/noRP/small/large, DoRothEA A-B, KD response. Pass if CORUM raw ≥ 6.4× (CLR) with DoRothEA within −0.005 of K10; fail if CORUM < K10 (3.51×) or DoRothEA −0.01. Then the M3D lab-split version on SC (M3D is the tuning set; exploratory) ~1 h. Confirmatory only on an untouched truth: **yeast Complex Portal complexes + YEASTRACT regulons on the Kemmeren deleteome wild types** (yeast truths are unconsumed per `step4_gateF_fail_report.md`; the gate-F failure was about mutant reproducibility, not WT; the PI's Rosetta warning does not apply to Kemmeren) or human Reactome/co-essentiality on K562 gwps (section 3). Power as in `reciprocity_heldout_design.md` (n_eff ≥ 100 small complexes).
- **Expected.** CORUM raw +0.02 to +0.05 over K10 (ribosome recovers; satellites demoted), small complexes +, DoRothEA −0.003 to 0, large loose regulons −0.005 to −0.01 (co-clustered only coarsely). Hub control should not erase it (co-clustering is not hub-driven).
- **Disguise risk: medium.** Hard units (C4b) cost regulons ×0.30; this is the soft, multi-resolution, half-consensus version, but if the gain evaporates when w_γ is uniform it is coarse-graining in disguise. Also a cousin of the reproducibility gate (which failed as a hard filter); the difference is that no row is ever silenced.

### 2. Cross-compendium partner-less gate (external-replicate symmetric sink)

- **Mechanism.** The repo's own conclusion is that the remaining lever is genes with no real partner spending a full budget on junk (`background_width_exploration.md` §1), and that the half-split detector loses power (halving N) and fails at f = 10%. Use an **independent compendium of the same cell line** as the replicate instead: K562 essential (half A), K562 genome-wide (1,553 profiles, 8,229 genes, on disk) and RPE1. ρ_i = P(i's top-20 CLR partners in compendium X overlap its top-20 in compendium Y more than chance; hypergeometric), soft. B_ij = A_ij ρ_i ρ_j, B_ii absorbs the rest (S1's form). No N is lost on the main compendium.
- **Label-free tuning.** k (20) and the chance rule fixed in advance; ρ is a p-value-to-weight map with no truth.
- **Cheapest test (~40 min, K562 in-sample).** CLR on gwps exists in `results/perturbseq_bulk_k562gwps`; compute ρ, rebuild B, walk, jackknife against K10 on the seven K562 truths. Pass: small complexes (3–6) ≥ +0.005 and DoRothEA ≥ −0.005. Diagnostic first: the fraction of genes with ρ < 0.5 should be near the 10.1% measured by the half-split (if it is 40%, the compendia differ too much and the idea dies here). Confirmatory on RPE1 built with a K562-derived gate (RPE1 CORUM was a key secondary once; label it) or on gwps with untouched truths.
- **Expected.** Small complexes ×1.3–1.8 (discounted from the synthetic ×2.6 oracle), ribosome unchanged (dilution is not the junk problem), regulons 0 to +; **E. coli: nothing** (0.3% partner-less; no second compendium is independent of M3D).
- **Disguise risk: low-medium.** It is S1 with a new detector; S1's mechanism was validated by oracle, the detector was the open problem. Fails if cross-compendium overlap is dominated by shared global programs (growth, stress), making everyone reproducible.

### 3. Adaptive depth by hub decile on a log grid (the PI's ask, in its best form)

- **What exists.** A per-gene k_i(t) diagnostic in the multiscale pilot (dropped: "not a module size at small t, hub-skewed π"), hub-stratified scoring at fixed t, and the size–depth diagnostic (t ∝ n^0.43; TF regulons best at t = 96 vs sigmulons 12 vs operons 4 on the log grid; DoRothEA 4 vs CollecTRI 48). No method chooses t per gene. Confirmed by grep.
- **Mechanism.** A row's reachable set grows like keff^t until it meets its module boundary, then polynomially (spectral dimension 1–1.5, `supplement.md`), so on log t each row has a knee; hubs mix in a few steps, peripheral regulon members slowly. One t serves neither. Best realisable form: **depth by hub decile.** Stratify genes by a *method-neutral* hub statistic (MI row mean, used in `robust_z`), grid t ∈ {1,2,3,4,6,8,12,16,24,32,48,64,96,128}, and choose t_d per decile by the existing lab-split criterion (`analysis/lab_splits.R`: AP of the train-half row of Pᵗ against the test half's top-1% CLR partners) **restricted to rows in decile d**. Readout S_ij = (P^{t_i}_ij + P^{t_j}_ji)/2. Ten parameters, not G. A per-gene variant (knee of log k_i(t), k_i = 1/Σ_j (Pᵗ_ij)², smoothed within decile) is the second form. **Control inside the same run:** PPR (Σ_t (1−α)α^t Pᵗ) and the heat kernel (Poisson weights), which mix all depths with one global parameter chosen by the same criterion; if decile depth does not beat them, it is not worth its complexity.
- **Label-free tuning.** Entirely the lab-split criterion; the hub statistic does not depend on the operator.
- **Cheapest test.** M3D: 8 splits × 2 directions × 14 depths; walks are seconds, the criterion is per gene already computable: ~1 h; then SC/all AUPR raw and hub-stratified (M3D exploratory). K562 ~4 h. Pass: hub-stratified SC +0.005 over t = 9 and raw not below −0.005. Confirmatory regulon truth that is untouched: **B. subtilis** (SubtiWiki regulons; Nicolas et al. 2012 tiling-array compendium or Arrieta-Ortiz et al. 2015, both public, < 1 GB **[verify availability and format]**), the natural sibling of RegulonDB; this is also the second organism the paper notes ask for.
- **Expected.** Hubs choose shallow t (concentrated rows, so their pairs rise in the pooled rank): raw pooled AUPR within ±0.01 of t = 9, with the real gain **hub-stratified**, +0.005 to +0.015 on SC. No CORUM effect (the ribosome's knee is at t ≈ 4–6 and the per-pair mass is still 0.9/77).
- **Disguise risk: medium.** If decile depths come out nearly equal (the global criterion is flat within 1% for t = 7–11), this collapses to t = 9. If hubs go shallow and the raw gain is hub-driven, it is the "hubs at large t" artefact in reverse. The per-decile knee must be checked against a rewired-operator null first (10 min) so that "adaptive" is not fitting noise.

### 4. Topology-gated hybrid readout on a common z scale

- **Mechanism.** The PI's "clique-like vs star-like" idea, made label-free: for gene i, c_i = weighted clustering coefficient of its top-10 attention neighbourhood (do my partners attend to each other?). High c = clique, low c = star/regulon. Score z_ij = g_ij · z_CLR,ij + (1 − g_ij) · z_a3r,ij, with g_ij = c_i c_j (or the max). Both terms are row-standardised z's, so they are commensurable; this avoids the rank conversion that destroyed CLR's CORUM lead.
- **Label-free tuning.** c_i has no parameter; the blend form is fixed in advance. A soft threshold on c can be set at the mode of its distribution.
- **Cheapest test (20 min, K562 in-sample).** a3r and CLR score matrices exist (`results/clr_logpt_test`); the blend is a line of R; jackknife on the seven truths. Pass: CORUM ≥ a3r (7.95×) *and* DoRothEA ≥ K10; fail if neither improves on a3r.
- **Expected.** CORUM +0.00 to +0.02 over a3r (ribosome rows get CLR's scale directly), regulons ≈ a3r. Modest, but interpretable: "attend only where the local graph is not already a clique".
- **Disguise risk: high.** It is a3r with a weight; the E. coli complex test of a3r was underpowered, not negative, and any hybrid inherits that power problem. Worth it only inside the same preregistration as idea 1 or 2 on an untouched truth.

### 5. Self-supervised learned attention on the compendium (idea and comparator)

- **Mechanism.** Train one attention layer (G queries/keys of dimension d ≈ 32, values = expression) with a masked-gene objective: predict each held-out gene's profile across samples from the others. Initialise logits at CLR z/τ (so the estimated attention is the starting point) and also from scratch. Read out the learned attention exactly like ours (softmax, walk, same t rule). This is the matched-data version of the scFM comparison: if learning the kernel from the same 907 arrays beats the MI kernel on held-out regulons, "estimated, not learned" is wrong. Label-free (self-supervised).
- **Tuning.** Early stopping on held-out *samples* (lab halves), never on truths.
- **Cheapest test.** 4,297 × 907 on a GTX 1080: minutes per epoch; a day for seeds and lab halves. Score on M3D (exploratory), confirm on B. subtilis or EcoMAC non-overlap arrays (post hoc only).
- **Expected.** Learned ≈ CLR-attention on regulons (Ahlmann-Eltze et al. 2025 found linear baselines match deep models in perturbation prediction; the same logic applies here), possibly below on CORUM (learned attention also spends a budget). A clean negative strengthens the paper's thesis; a positive is a bigger paper.
- **Disguise risk: low.** Nothing like it has been run; the scFM arms used foreign pretraining.

### 6. Non-backtracking walk

- **Mechanism.** π is hub-skewed (max/median 310× on M3D, 18,457× on K562), the known localisation pathology of walks on sparse graphs; the non-backtracking operator (Krzakala et al. 2013, PNAS) avoids localisation on hubs and is the standard fix for spectral resolution in sparse graphs. On the directed, weighted top-50 graph the NB matrix is 2E × 2E (~0.6M states for K562), sparse; t-step NB mass from i to j is a sparse product chain.
- **Tuning.** t by the lab-split criterion (NB walks need a smaller t). No new parameter.
- **Cheapest test.** 1–2 h implementation, 30 min run on M3D. Pass: hub-stratified SC ≥ t = 9 attention and raw not below; the interesting endpoint is the *all-evidence* truth where the hub-controlled lead is small (+0.004 to +0.008).
- **Expected.** Small (+0.005) on hub-controlled regulon truths; nothing on CORUM (dilution is not backtracking).
- **Disguise risk: low-medium.** The lazy self-loop already damps backtracking; the NB gain may vanish at α = 0.5.

### 7. Soft MCL inflation between walk steps

- **Mechanism.** Markov clustering (van Dongen 2000; Enright et al. 2002, built for complexes) alternates expansion P² with entrywise inflation (power r, renormalise). A gentle r ∈ {1.1, 1.2, 1.5} after each of the t steps suppresses the perplexity-floor leak of small cliques (the repo's small-complex mechanism) without hard clustering.
- **Tuning.** r by the lab-split criterion jointly with t.
- **Cheapest test.** 20 min K562 in-sample on small (3–6) and merged noRP complexes, DoRothEA as the guard.
- **Expected.** Small complexes ×1.2–1.5, large unchanged (inflation keeps an exchangeable block uniform), regulons −0.005 (loose spread is sharpened away), **satellites get worse** (a 0.84 one-sided row becomes ~1.0), so CORUM raw may fall.
- **Disguise risk: high.** It targets exactly what reciprocity targets, with a parameter instead of none; if it does not beat reciprocity on small complexes in-sample it should be dropped.

### 8. De-indirecting before attention, for the directed-edge endpoint only

- **Mechanism.** ARACNE's DPI and network deconvolution (Feizi et al. 2013) remove transitive edges; inside a clique every edge is "indirect", so they are poison for complexes, but they address the paper's weak point (attention last of three on direct TF–target pairs; GENIE3/GRNBoost2 lead DREAM5 Y-E). Apply ND to the CLR matrix, then softmax and walk at small t, and read out the directed C3 latent-column score.
- **Tuning.** ND's β by the lab-split criterion on directed held-out partner agreement.
- **Cheapest test.** DREAM5 net1/net3 Y-E are touched (descriptive only), 1 h. Confirmatory: B. subtilis directed edges, inside the same preregistration as idea 3.
- **Expected.** Y-E +10–20% relative AUPR; co-membership −; CORUM −.
- **Disguise risk: low** for the edge endpoint; irrelevant to CORUM, listed because the PI asked.

**Not proposed, and why:** glasso/precision (ridge partial correlation was the weakest BEELINE baseline, and a clique is a dense precision block); diffusion-map α-normalisation and Laplacian eigenmaps (π-normalised readouts failed by the lift mechanism; the spectral truncation of Pᵗ reproduces the 1/n block entry exactly); self-tuning kernels (per-gene width = O2, failed); Efron null on rows (tested eight ways); return-probability normalisation Pᵗ_ij/√(Pᵗ_ii Pᵗ_jj) (a one-line idea that I worked through: it is n-invariant in a block but inflates junk rows by 1/(½)ᵗ, the same failure as the lift, so I withdraw it).

## 3. Comparator recommendation for (b)

**Requirement:** a comparator that already beat us somewhere, data on disk or a small public download, a preregistered endpoint under which we can lose, and a conclusion worth having either way. The scPRINT weakness was that its attention equals its permutation control, so a win proved nothing. The one scFM readout that *did* beat us is the scGPT token-embedding cosine (n = 2, STRING-driven, `scfm_scoring.md`). That is the comparator to take seriously, and the honest framing is **prior versus inference**: a token embedding is data-independent knowledge; our network is inferred from this cell line's perturbations.

### Primary: incremental value of inferred attention over learned gene-embedding priors, K562 genome-wide compendium, two untouched truths

- **Compared.** (A) scGPT whole-human token-embedding cosine (on disk); (B) Geneformer V2-104M contextual gene embeddings from K562 cells (K562 essential raw single cells are on disk, 10 GB; forward pass only, fits a GTX 1080) **[verify V2 checkpoint size]**; (C) ESM2/GenePT cosine as the data-free floor (already used as a control); (D) CLR-NS and CLR attention (t = 9, transferred) on the gwps pseudobulk (1,553 profiles, 8,229 genes; network exists in `results/perturbseq_bulk_k562gwps`); (E) hybrids: rank-average and Stouffer of (A)+(D), (B)+(D).
- **Truths (new to the project).** Reactome pathway co-membership (public, msigdb C2:CP:REACTOME, small) and **DepMap co-essentiality modules** (Wainberg et al. 2021, Nat Genet **[verify]**; public CSV, small) as the generic truths; the gwps **knockdown-response modules from half B** as the cell-specific truth (scored descriptively once on gwps: disclose). CORUM is not used (in-sample everywhere).
- **Preregistered endpoints.** P1: AUPR(E) − AUPR(best prior) on the cell-specific truth ≥ +0.005 (superiority, paired delete-half gene jackknife, one-sided 0.025). P2: AUPR(D) − AUPR(best prior) on co-essentiality, non-inferiority at margin −0.01. Secondary: same on Reactome; hub-stratified versions; prior-only vs permuted-expression control for (B) (if (B) equals its permutation control, it is a prior and goes in arm A).
- **Expected.** Priors win on Reactome (generic pathway knowledge; they beat us on STRING for the same reason). On co-essentiality the data-driven network should be close (co-essentiality is a functional-module truth in the same cell-line family). On the cell-specific truth the inferred network should win and the hybrid should beat both, because the prior cannot know which knockdowns move which genes in K562.
- **If we lose** (hybrid ≈ prior on the cell-specific truth): data inference adds nothing a 2024 prior does not already encode for this cell line; the paper's claim must retreat to bacteria, where no prior exists. That is a real loss condition and a publishable one.
- **Cost.** Geneformer forward pass on ~100k K562 cells: a few GPU-hours; the rest is CPU minutes; 1–2 weeks of work including preregistration; < 5 GB new disk.

## 4. Three most surprising or highest-variance ideas

1. **Self-supervised learned attention on the compendium (idea 5).** Either outcome moves the paper: a tie is the strongest possible support for "estimated attention suffices"; a win would mean the kernel, not the architecture, is where learning helps, and the paper becomes about a label-free trainable CLR.
2. **Attend over perturbations, not genes (bipartite walk).** In Perturb-seq pseudobulk the columns are knockdowns. Let genes attend to perturbations (which knockdowns move me?) and perturbations attend to genes, and walk on the bipartite graph; the perturbation side is normalised by its own degree, so a ribosome knockdown that moves 3,000 genes is a hub that gets damped, and two genes are co-members if they are reached through the same perturbations. This is a degree-corrected estimator of "shared regulators" built from the experimental design itself, and it is the natural bridge to paper 2 (differential CLR). Falsifiable in-sample on K562 KD-response (where attention currently loses by 0.10) in ~1 h; expected to tie CLR there and to say nothing about CORUM.
3. **Time as the score: first-passage depth on the log grid.** Score a pair by the smallest t at which Pᵗ_ij exceeds a fixed multiple of π_j, symmetrised by the *later* of the two directions. Distances are n-invariant inside a clique (everyone is reached by t ≈ 2) and satellites are reached late from the clique side. It is a diffusion-distance readout (cousin of Cao et al. 2013, diffusion state distance, built for hub-robust PPI function prediction). High variance because junk mutual pairs are also "early"; it only works on top of a junk-row gate (idea 2), which is why it is here and not in the ranked list.
