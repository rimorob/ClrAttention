# Review: adaptive, label-free setting of aCLR / tcaCLR breadth and depth

Hats worn: senior computer scientist (graph diffusion, spectral methods, scalable linear algebra) and senior statistician (empirical Bayes, model selection, resampling). Reviewer: Claude Fable 5.1. Read-only review: no compute was run, no truth opened, no existing file edited. Sources read: the depth-grid plan, the theory memo (header and Q1), the simulation reading, the background-width memo (verdict section), the keff verdict table. I did NOT read in full: the preregistration, corum_expert2, the depth design review, calibrate.R, or the code of the second CLR. Claims about those are marked in "Things I could not verify".

## Executive summary

1. The plan's three stage-1 criteria are biased in known, opposite directions: C1 (agree with the other half's CLR) favours shallow settings by construction; C2 (self-reproducibility) rewards smoothness and hub-dominated, near-stationary scores, so it rises with t and keff; C3's penalty (0.5 k log n, k = reachable entries per row) is not a parameter count and is a function of t, so it penalises depth mechanically. The rule "declare only if all three agree within one step" will most likely fail for reasons that are artefacts, so the "finding" would be uninformative.
2. The 448-cell grid is partly redundant: clipO (clip at the output, after Stouffer) is monotone, so it cannot change any AUPR except through ties. Neighbouring half-octave cells are almost the same estimate. Argmax of a median over 8 correlated splits will pick up winner's curse; use a smoothed response surface and a one-standard-error rule.
3. Best statistical reformulation (candidate 1): the walk P^t is a low-pass filter on the graph spectrum, and the graph is a model for the covariance (or precision) of expression across experiments. Then depth and width have a held-out Gaussian-copula marginal likelihood in the eigenbasis, with an explicit parameter count, and a random-matrix noise edge (from per-gene permuted data) gives t directly: t* = ln(eps) / ln(lambda_edge).
4. Candidate 2: the second CLR on log P^t is a row-standardised diffusion PMI (log P^t_ij / pi_j with Varadhan's log P ~ -d^2/4tau reading). Replace the reachable-only row statistics with the stationary (degree-corrected) null; the project's own simulation found the lift the best readout.
5. keff: it is a bias-variance bandwidth, not a count of true partners. That is why O2 (local FDR counting detectable partners; permutation null not pivotal, pi0 = 1.000, floor at 2) lost, and why oracle per-gene widths did not help in simulation. The data prefer wide attention because noisy top-k selection scatters true partners over ranks. Set keff by blocked cross-validation with the one-standard-error rule, with a StARS-style instability curve as a second reading.
6. The PI's island idea (conductance, personalised-PageRank sweep, network community profile) is sound as a diagnostic and as a depth setter (t between 1/spectral-gap-within and 1/conductance-out), but not as a stand-alone keff setter: it needs a sparsified graph, so it inherits keff; map-equation code lengths are not comparable across different sparsifications; and the project's oracle-width simulation already says per-gene island size as keff does not help except for tiny complexes.
7. Validation without a fresh truth: semi-synthetic planted modules with a dose-response check (the chosen setting must move with planted size, N and noise), per-gene permuted data as negative control (rule must return "no structure"), a pre-written regret rule, and subsampling real data to a range of N and G. Matching keff 10 / t 9 on M3D and K562 is necessary, not sufficient (those were tuned after many looks).
8. If one thing runs: the spectral-edge rule plus the blocked-CV graph-signal likelihood on M3D and K562, together with the planted dose-response simulation. All of it is under an hour or two of compute.

## 1. Critical review of the plan

### 1.1 Criteria

**C1 (AUPR of half 1's score against the top 1% of half 2's CLR).**
- Circular towards CLR. The pseudo-truth is the output of the t = 1-like baseline; the t = 1 identity (memo) makes a shallow tcaCLR nearly CLR. C1 therefore peaks shallow. It measures "similarity to CLR", not "closeness to biology". It was used to pick t = 9 for aCLR, which makes it a criterion with a history of influence on the registered setting.
- The top 1% of CLR is hub- and batch-dominated (CLR is not hub-neutral, memo 1.2). On K562 random halves the two halves share guides, cell-cycle state and sequencing batches, so replicated dependence includes nuisance dependence that is not biology. Splitting M3D by laboratory is better; K562 has no equivalent block, so use blocks by knockdown groups (for example clusters of knockdowns by guide library or by perturbation-profile cluster) if any exist, otherwise state the limitation.
- AUPR against a pseudo-truth defined by the same half-2 data has base rate 1% by construction; fine, but report a hub-matched version (see 1.4).

**C2 (tcaCLR half 1 vs tcaCLR half 2, top 1%).**
- Reproducibility is not accuracy. As t grows, P^t tends to the stationary distribution pi_j proportional to weighted degree; the score becomes a rank-one degree ranking, which is perfectly reproducible. The same holds for large keff. C2 is therefore monotone in depth and width up to the point where reachable-only row statistics break (reachability confinement). The criterion needs a null-adjusted form: reproducibility of the method minus reproducibility of a degree-only surrogate (score_ij = pi_i pi_j or the stationary lift), or reproducibility computed after residualising hub effects.

**C3 (mean log P1^t[i,j] over half-2 top-1% CLR pairs).**
- Target is again the top-1% of CLR (same circularity as C1), and it is hub-weighted: rows with many held-out edges dominate the mean. Use row-balanced weights or a proper multinomial per row.
- Unreachable held-out edges give -infinity. The plan normalises over reachable entries and so must handle this: use a mixture floor P_mix = (1 - eps) P^t + eps / G, and then eps is itself a parameter; report the unreachable fraction as a separate number.
- Penalty. 0.5 k log n is the BIC penalty for k fitted parameters on n observations. Here the cell has zero fitted parameters; the hyper-parameters (keff, cap, t, laziness, clip) are about 3 to 5 numbers. k = mean reachable entries per row is the support size, not model complexity, and it grows with t, so the penalty is a deterministic, monotone cost on depth that is unrelated to overfitting. It also double-counts: a held-out likelihood already penalises over-flexibility. Dimensionally, 0.5 k log n is a total, while "mean of log P" is a per-pair mean; subtract it only after multiplying by n. Use either blocked held-out likelihood (no penalty) or a penalised in-sample likelihood with n = effective sample size and k = number of free hyper-parameters (see section 3), not both.

### 1.2 Declare-or-not rule
- "C1, C2, C3 within one grid step of each other" is built on three criteria with known, different biases (shallow, deep, depth-penalised). Disagreement is expected and says nothing about the dataset. Neighbouring cells are half an octave apart, so "within one step" is a weak notion in one direction and an arbitrary threshold in the other. Also keff and t trade off along a ridge (the effective spread of the walk grows with both), so axis-wise agreement is the wrong geometry. Compare cells in terms of a reparametrisation: effective spatial scale s = median over rows of the entropy of P^t_i (or the participation ratio sum P^2), and compare selections as pairs (s, keff) or by the difference in stage-2-independent performance metrics, not by grid index.

### 1.3 Multiplicity and dependence between cells
- 448 cells (really 224 distinct for rank metrics, see 1.5) with argmax of a median over 8 splits: winner's curse, with splits that are not independent (the K562 random halves reuse the same knockdowns; the M3D lab splits are only the first 8 stored). The plateau of two neighbours in each axis is a visual device, not an uncertainty statement.
- Replace by: (a) fit a low-order smooth surface (quadratic in log t and log keff, or a thin-plate spline) to the per-split criterion values across all cells; (b) take the argmax of the fitted surface; (c) report the one-standard-error region, with the SE computed by a block bootstrap over laboratories (M3D) or knockdown clusters (K562), and choose the most parsimonious cell in that region (smallest t and smallest keff, the cheaper operator). This is the standard cross-validation 1-SE rule and plays the role of the regulariser.

### 1.4 Hub effects
- CLR scores carry (h_i - h_bar) + (h_j - h_bar) terms. Any criterion built on the top 1% of pairs samples the hubs. Add a hub-stratified reading: restrict C1 to pairs where both genes lie in the middle 80% of row-SD, or report C1 in degree strata. Also report the correlation of the cell's score with log degree product.

### 1.5 Grid structure
- clipO: the plan says "second CLR's row z over reachable pairs, combined by Stouffer, clipped at 0 or not". If the clip is applied after the Stouffer combination it is a monotone map and changes no ranking metric except through ties at 0 (and the ap_fast tie convention can then change AUPR). It is a no-op for every criterion in the plan; drop it from the grid, or if the intended clip is on the one-sided Z before combination, say so in the plan. I could not verify from the code which one it is (the grep in tc_lib.R shows no clip call; the clipping is probably in the R6 class, which I did not read).
- clipS (clip before Stouffer in the pairwise score) changes both the candidate set and the weights fed to the softmax, but the softmax only uses positive scores among the top candidates, so clipping only matters through (z_ij + z_ji) when one direction is negative. A two-level factor evaluated at two or three keff values is enough; a full cross over 7 keff is not needed.
- Depth 192 and 256 are near-stationary for a lazy walk on a graph with 10-50 neighbours; show a mixing diagnostic (total-variation distance to stationary, or the spectral gap) and cap the grid where the reachable-only second CLR collapses. Add the small end (t = 2, 3 are there) and replace 192, 256 by 13, 18 to resolve the region around 9.
- Cost: sequential P^t to t = 256 on a 9,622 x 9,622 dense matrix is about 256 x 1.8 TFlop per operator, 14 operators about 6e15 flop, roughly 1.5 to 3 hours at realistic dense throughput on 40 cores. Feasible but wasteful; early powers are sparse (cap 150), and the spectral route (section 2) gives all depths from one eigendecomposition.

### 1.6 Stage 2 on spent truths
- Descriptive use is fine and the "never overrides stage 1" rule is right. I would add three things:
  1. Regret: AUPR(label-free cell) / AUPR(truth-best cell) per truth. A label-free rule is useful if regret is small everywhere, not if it wins somewhere.
  2. Criterion validity: the Spearman correlation across cells between each stage-1 criterion and each spent-truth AUPR. That is a far stronger descriptive use of spent truths than selecting a cell: it asks whether the surrogate tracks accuracy over a 224-cell landscape. Caveat: the criteria were designed after the project saw these truths, so this is a diagnostic, not a test.
  3. The reading "unclipped better if not lower in more than one truth" is loose; replace by a paired comparison with a block-bootstrap interval on the per-truth difference, and say that with four truths there is no inference, only a description.
- A sum of truths dominated by large complexes (HepG2 gain carried by three large complexes) means pooled AUPR is a statement about ribosome-like structure. Report AUPR by complex-size stratum (3-6, 7-20, 21-50, 50+) in stage 2, since the simulation says the method's sign flips with module size.

### 1.7 What I would change before it runs
See the list at the end ("Changes to the plan before it runs").

## 2. Better statistical formulations

### 2.1 What aCLR / tcaCLR are, in statistical terms
- CLR: row-wise empirical-null studentisation of MI (Efron-style z without density estimation); invariant to row-wise affine distortions of D.
- aCLR attention: p_ij proportional to exp(S_ij / tau_i) with tau_i solving perplexity = keff. This is the t-SNE perplexity calibration (Hinton and Roweis 2002) and is the maximum-entropy distribution over partners subject to a mean-score constraint; the perplexity fixes the Lagrange multiplier. It has no probabilistic meaning for "number of partners". An honest Bayesian reading (keff_i = sum_j posterior probability that j is a partner) is O2's, which lost.
- Lazy walk: P = 0.5 I + 0.5 A. With laziness 0.5 all eigenvalues of the symmetrised operator are non-negative, so P^t is a valid positive-semidefinite kernel for every t; 0.5 is the minimal laziness with that property. That is the one principled justification for laziness and it also shows laziness is not a free parameter (it only rescales time: lazy walk with weight alpha on the current node is approximately the heat kernel exp(-tau L) with tau about (1 - alpha) t at large t).
- P^t is a spectral low-pass filter: with symmetric normalised operator spectrum mu_k and lazy eigenvalues lambda_k = (1 + mu_k)/2, P^t = sum_k lambda_k^t phi_k psi_k^T. Depth t sets the cutoff: modes with lambda_k^t above a threshold survive.
- Second CLR on log P^t: by Varadhan's formula, -4 tau log K_tau(i,j) tends to d(i,j)^2, so log P^t_ij is, up to scale, a negative squared diffusion distance in the log domain; the row z-score is the row-standardised distance. More usefully, log(P^t_ij / pi_j) is the diffusion pointwise mutual information (PMI), which is what DeepWalk / NetMF factorise (Levy and Goldberg 2014; Qiu et al. 2018, NetMF) [verify details]. The row z-score of log P^t over reachable entries therefore uses as null the row's own reachable-ball distribution of log-distances, which is ill-defined: the number of nodes at distance d grows geometrically, so the row histogram is dominated by the far shell and in closed modules the reachable ball is the module itself. That is exactly the "reachability confinement" the simulation found.

### 2.2 Candidate 1 (recommended): graph-signal Gaussian model with the walk as a spectral filter, scale estimated by marginal or held-out likelihood
Model. Let x_n in R^G be the (rank-normal transformed, so a Gaussian copula) expression profile of experiment n. Assume x_n ~ N(0, Sigma) with
 Sigma = U diag(f(mu_k)) U^T + sigma^2 I,
where U, mu_k are the eigenvectors and eigenvalues of the symmetrised attention operator built from half 1 (the data used to build the graph), and f is a spectral filter: f(mu) = c * ((1 + mu)/2)^t (the walk), or c * (1 - beta mu)^-1 (resolvent, simultaneous autoregressive / CAR covariance), or c * exp(-tau (1 - mu)) (heat kernel). The sparse-precision alternative is Q = (I - beta A)^T (I - beta A) / sigma^2.
Estimation. On the held-out experiments (half 2, or blocked folds), the Gaussian log-likelihood depends on the data only through the spectral energy e_k = (1/N2) sum_n (u_k^T x_n)^2:
 loglik(f, sigma^2) = -(N2/2) sum_k [ log(f_k + sigma^2) + e_k / (f_k + sigma^2) ] + const.
So for every (keff, f-family, scale) the criterion is a one-dimensional function of the energy-versus-frequency profile; fitting t is a log-linear regression of e_k - sigma^2 on log lambda_k. sigma^2 is the noise floor, estimated from the trailing eigenmodes.
Parameter count and penalty. Free parameters: (c, t or tau or beta, sigma^2) = 3. Held-out likelihood needs no penalty. If one prefers an in-sample criterion, BIC = -2 loglik + 3 log N_eff, with N_eff the effective number of independent experiments (section 3.0), NOT the number of pairs. The graph-selection (keff, cap) is a hyper-parameter chosen on half 1 and scored on half 2, so no double dipping.
Why it is statistically meaningful. (i) Each parameter has a likelihood and a parameter count; (ii) the energy profile is the data's own statement of which graph frequencies carry variance; (iii) it makes keff, t and clip choices comparable across datasets with one scale; (iv) no truth. t* from the spectral cutoff is also available as a plain rule: with lambda_edge the largest eigenvalue of the same operator built from per-gene-permuted data (the noise edge; Wigner/Marchenko-Pastur-type), choose t such that lambda_edge^t <= eps (for example 0.05) and compute how many signal modes survive (lambda^t > 0.5): t* = ln(eps) / ln(lambda_edge). Self-consistency check: lambda^9 = 0.5 corresponds to lambda = 0.926, mu = 0.852; if the permuted-null edge for M3D has lambda_edge near 0.72 (so 0.72^9 = 0.05) the rule agrees with t = 9. I did not compute this.
Cost. One eigendecomposition per keff value (dense eigh on 9,622 is roughly 10^13 flop, 10 to 30 minutes on 56 cores; or truncated top 1,500 to 2,000 modes with ARPACK in minutes on the sparse operator, with the tail energy from trace(S) minus the sum of the captured e_k). Projection X2 U is N x G x r, about 10^11 flop. For 7 keff values about 1 to 2 hours dense or minutes truncated. All depths and families from one decomposition.
Weaknesses (be sceptical). (a) Gaussian-copula covariance describes linear-monotone dependence; MI-based CLR targets nonlinear dependence. (b) Marginal-variance modes mix batch and biology; the held-out blocks must be laboratories. (c) Energy on graph frequencies measures whether the graph explains covariance, which rewards big low-frequency modes (global programs), not necessarily complexes. (d) With N << G the likelihood is carried by a few modes; fine for choosing a scale, weak for fine structure. (e) It is my proposal and untested here.

### 2.3 Candidate 2: standardised diffusion PMI with a degree-corrected null
Replace log P^t row-standardised over reachable entries by
 W_ij = log( P^t_ij / pi_j ), pi_j the stationary mass (proportional to weighted degree), floor at log(eps pi_j) for unreachable pairs,
standardised with a robust, global null: central matching (Efron) on the histogram of W over all pairs, or row median and MAD over all entries including floored ones. This is (a) a PMI, with a null (independence, P^t_ij = pi_j) that is the degree-corrected configuration-model expectation, hence hub-neutral by construction; (b) defined without the reachable-only rule, so no confinement; (c) closed form from the same P^t. The simulation reading found that lifts were the best readouts, which is consistent with this. Test it in the same simulation that refuted size invariance. The second CLR becomes a studentised PMI; the Stouffer combination across directions is then (W_ij/sigma_i + W_ji/sigma_j)/sqrt(2) as before.

### 2.4 Other formulations considered
- Two-group / local FDR on S: gives calibrated edge probabilities but, with the non-pivotal null, was the failure of O2 (section 3.7).
- Stochastic block / latent space model: the right model for modules, with BIC/ICL for the number of blocks; the walk's depth then equals the block relaxation time. Planted-partition fitting is too costly to run per dataset at 10^4 nodes (spectral SBM via the eigenmodes above the noise edge is the cheap version and coincides with the spectral rule above).
- Gaussian graphical model (precision) view: the resolvent (I - beta A)^-1 is the SAR covariance; a GGM on 5,000 to 10,000 genes with N = 400 to 1,300 needs strong regularisation (graphical lasso, partial correlations are not identified at N < G); network deconvolution is the direct-vs-indirect analogue but it targets direct edges, not modules.
- Resolvent / PageRank: R = (1 - beta)(I - beta A)^-1 is a geometric mixture of depths with mean walk length beta/(1 - beta); heat kernel is a Poisson mixture. Both have one interpretable scale and smoother depth profiles than a fixed t, and no parity problem. Dense output costs G sparse solves, no cheaper than walking; in the eigenbasis (candidate 1) they are free. A mixture over depths is also a hedge against the multi-scale structure in section 3.7.

## 3. Setting every parameter label-free

### 3.0 Effective sample size and the unit of resampling
Pairs are not independent: 10^7 pairs share 10^4 genes and 10^3 experiments. Two effective sizes matter.
- Experiments: N_eff = (tr C)^2 / tr(C^2), the participation ratio of the N x N sample correlation matrix C of experiments (computed on a random subset of genes if needed). With lab structure N_eff is a fraction of N; I would expect a few hundred. Use it in any n of a penalty and to size the noise floor.
- Genes: G_eff = (tr R)^2 / tr(R^2) with R the gene-gene correlation of CLR rows, or the number of eigenmodes above the noise edge; pairs-effective is of the order G_eff, not G^2.
- Resampling unit: blocks of experiments (laboratory for M3D; knockdown clusters for K562; cell-line or study for compendia of human data), never random experiments if blocks exist. For gene-level quantities use a gene block bootstrap (resample genes together with their rows and columns) to get intervals on any rule's output.

### 3.1 Held-out likelihood versus BIC here
- BIC approximates a marginal likelihood when one of the candidate models is correct and n is the number of independent observations; its penalty is 0.5 k log n_eff. Here every candidate is misspecified, parameters are hyper-parameters (3 to 5), and n is ill-defined. The penalty is tiny relative to likelihood differences when n is the pair count, and arbitrary when it is N_eff.
- Blocked K-fold cross-validated likelihood estimates out-of-sample predictive loss for new experiments, which is the quantity that matters, and is AIC-equivalent asymptotically (Stone 1977). Use blocked CV (K = 5 to 8 laboratory or cluster blocks) with the 1-SE rule as the regulariser. Keep a BIC with n = N_eff only as a second reading (it should agree on the ordering of t; if they differ, say so).
- Do not apply penalties to a held-out score.

### 3.2 keff (breadth)
- Estimator: choose keff by blocked-CV graph-signal likelihood (candidate 1) maximised over a log grid 7 to 80, with the 1-SE rule toward smaller keff.
- Second reading, StARS-style instability (Liu, Roeder, Wasserman 2010): draw subsamples of experiments of size b = about 10 sqrt(N_eff) (blocked), rebuild the top-k partner sets for each gene, and compute instability D(k) = mean over rows and pairs of 2 theta (1 - theta), theta the selection frequency. Choose the smallest k with D(k) at most 0.05. This is the resampling analogue of BIC and is known to over-select (dense), which is consistent with the data's preference for wide attention. Cost: MI recomputation per subsample; the project has per-split MI already for 8 splits; 20 subsamples is of the order of the existing full-MI cost times 20 (hours). Use rank correlation of the CLR rows instead of MI for the subsamples if cost matters, and say so.
- Penalty/regulariser: none beyond the 1-SE rule and the held-out evaluation; the "regularisation" is that large keff costs held-out likelihood once the filter over-smooths.
- Identifiability: width and depth trade off (the same effective spread); estimate on a 2-D surface and report the ridge. Expected: E. coli (4,297 genes, 466 experiments, sparse regulon hubs, strong lab structure) at small to moderate keff; human (large complexes, ribosome-like cliques, strong global programs) wider. This is a prediction, not a result.
- Cheap sanity check: keff recovered on permuted data should not be interior (flat likelihood, rule flags "no structure").
- Failure modes: batch dependence counted as biology; heavy-tailed module sizes (one keff for all, section 3.7).

### 3.3 cap
- cap is a computational truncation, not a statistical parameter. Set cap = smallest c such that the tempered softmax mass outside the top c is below 1% in at least 95% of rows (in practice about 3 to 5 times keff). Report the realised tail mass. The plan's cap 150 for keff up to 56 should be checked with this criterion (at keff 56 about 2.7 times keff; likely marginal). Drop cap from the search.

### 3.4 Depth t (or tau, beta)
- Estimator: (i) spectral rule t* = ln(eps) / ln(lambda_edge), with lambda_edge from per-gene permuted data (10 permutations; edge = the median of the largest non-trivial eigenvalues); (ii) the energy-profile regression of candidate 1; (iii) the leak-window rule of section 5: t in [1 / gap_within, 1 / phi_out].
- Uses: the spectrum of the graph built from the data (a marginal property of the whole matrix).
- Penalty: model order is the number of modes above the noise edge (BIC/Bai-Ng/parallel-analysis style); the penalty is built into using the permuted null as the model-order criterion.
- Identifiability: t is weakly identified when the spectrum has no gap (continuous spectrum, as in real regulons); report the curvature of the held-out likelihood and the flatness range. If the curvature is flat across a factor of two, say so.
- Expected: E. coli and human may differ in lambda_edge because of N/G (about 0.11 to 0.13 in M3D and K562; HepG2 not checked) and in signal strength. I cannot predict the sign.
- Cheap sanity check: the rule should return a depth that scales with the planted module size in simulation.
- Failure modes: reachability confinement as t grows (the second CLR breaks before the spectral rule says stop): add an upper limit t_max where the reachable fraction of a typical row falls below 0.9 or the within-module retention exceeds 0.9.

### 3.5 Laziness
Fix at 0.5 (minimal for a positive-semidefinite kernel; otherwise redundant with t). Do not estimate.

### 3.6 MI estimator bin count and spline order
- For histogram or B-spline MI, the null MI of independent genes has mean about (b - 1)^2 / (2N) and sd about (b - 1) / (sqrt(2) N) (chi-square with (b - 1)^2 df, scaled), while a linear dependence with correlation rho contributes about rho^2 / 2. The row z-score absorbs the mean bias; variance grows linearly with b; resolution of nonlinear dependence grows with b. A label-free rule: choose b in 5 to 12 starting from b = round(N_eff^(1/3)) and keep the value maximising blocked split-half reproducibility of the row ranks of D (after hub adjustment), with the 1-SE rule toward smaller b. Weakly identified; the impact is small relative to keff and t, and I would fix b = 10 unless the check disagrees by more than 1 SE. (My constants are derived for a binned estimator under independence; for B-splines they differ by a constant factor.)
- Alternative that sidesteps binning: Gaussian-copula (rank-normal) transform followed by Gaussian MI -0.5 log(1 - r^2), no bins; linear-only. Useful as the likelihood-side object in candidate 1.

### 3.7 Clip or no clip
- Output clip (after Stouffer): no effect on rankings; skip.
- Pairwise clip before Stouffer: for MI (non-negative, below-average values are noise), negative z carries no signal if the left tail of the pooled z histogram mirrors the fitted null (Efron central matching: fit N(delta0, sigma0) to the centre, compare the left tail). If it does, clipping is harmless and removes noise; if the left tail is heavier than the null, it carries dispersion information and should be kept. The 2007 Euclidean combination needs clipping because squares lose the sign; Stouffer does not. Make it a diagnostic, not a search axis.

### 3.8 Why O2 (local FDR width) probably lost, and whether a better empirical-Bayes rule exists
- Reasons, from the width memo [ran] and the verdict table: the permutation null is the null of no dependence, not pivotal on the CLR scale (real rows inflate their own SD), so pi0 = 1.000 and keff sat at the floor of 2; and even a perfect width does not repair large complexes (mass conservation: pair mass inside a mixed n-gene clique is m / (n - 1) at any width) and costs 20-gene complexes (x0.29 to x0.53).
- Deeper reason: lfdr counts statistically detectable partners; the smoothing bandwidth that predicts best is a bias-variance trade-off set by noise, as in kernel density estimation where the optimal bandwidth is not the number of modes. With a noisy score (N = 400 to 1,300) the true partners of a gene scatter over ranks well beyond the top few, so a wide softmax averages over the rank uncertainty. The data's preference for wide attention (K25 wins on K562 CORUM and M3D regulons; held-out search hit its edge at 40) is what this predicts.
- A differently built rule: estimate keff from the rank uncertainty of true partners: the split-half rank displacement of each gene's strongest partners (the k at which the top-k sets reproduce with overlap above a plateau) is the StARS/stability quantity above, and the width of the softmax should be of that size. It uses no null and no count of significant partners. The "empirical marginal" the PI mentions would be the Efron central-matching null on the CLR scale used as a background for a posterior, but the width problem is not a testing problem; I would not rebuild O2.
- Degree prior view: keff as an empirical-Bayes prior on node degree (Poisson or negative binomial with mean estimated from the global 1 - pi0) would give the same detectability count and the same failure; a heavy-tailed degree prior (power law) makes the per-gene keff multi-scale, but the oracle-width simulation says that per-gene exact widths do not help except for tiny complexes.

## 4. Validation without a fresh truth

1. Semi-synthetic planted structure on real null structure. Start from M3D and K562 matrices; destroy dependence by permuting each gene's values across experiments independently (keeps marginals, sparsity, zeros), then plant modules with sizes from a heavy-tailed distribution (3 to 200 genes), strengths lambda in {0.3, 0.55, 0.85}, overlapping membership, linear and nonlinear (monotone and U-shaped) links, hub genes, laboratory-structured shared factors, and N and G varied by subsampling. Use residual noise from real data after removing the top principal components. Simulate from at least one family different from the one the rule assumes (not Gaussian-field data if candidate 1 is used; use an LFR-like overlapping benchmark and a nonlinear generator), otherwise the check is circular.
2. Dose-response. A good rule's chosen (keff, t) must move with the planted size distribution, N and signal strength in the direction of the oracle best cell (the simulation showed tcaCLR's gain depends on module size, so oracle cells change). Report rank correlation of chosen scale with oracle scale across simulations, and regret = AUPR(oracle) - AUPR(chosen), relative, with an interval.
3. Self-consistency on real data. Does the rule return approximately keff 10 and t 9 on M3D and K562 without being fitted to them? That is necessary, not sufficient: those values were chosen after many looks on these matrices. Stronger: subsample experiments (N = 150, 250, 400, 700) and genes (G = 2,000 to full): the chosen scale should vary smoothly and in the predicted direction, and the chosen setting on a half should predict the chosen setting on the other half (stability of the rule, with interval from gene-block bootstrap).
4. Negative controls. (a) Per-gene permuted data: the rule must flag "no structure" (no eigenvalue above the null edge, held-out likelihood gain over t = 0 below zero) in at least 95% of permutations; (b) graph from dataset A applied to expression from dataset B (gene labels shuffled, or species-mismatched): likelihood gain must vanish; (c) shuffled gene labels within the held-out half.
5. Pre-written decision rule (suggested): the rule is declared useful if, on the simulation panel, (i) regret at most 5% of oracle AUPR in at least 80% of configurations; (ii) selected scale monotone in planted size (Spearman at least 0.7); (iii) negative controls flag at least 95%; (iv) on real data stable between halves within one grid step in at least 7 of 8 splits. Without all four, no label-free setting is declared.
6. What would convince me: (1)-(5) all met, plus small regret on the spent truths for the label-free cell (a descriptive reading, not a claim), plus a flat-versus-curved likelihood report so that "t unidentifiable here" can be said. What would not: matching 9 and 10 on M3D and K562; the label-free cell having the best AUPR on one spent truth; a gain from a rule selected in a 448-cell grid; success on simulations from the same family as the likelihood; agreement among criteria that share the CLR pseudo-truth.
7. Unspent truths. I do not know which exist. Candidates to check (unverified): DepMap co-essentiality modules for K562 (a different assay; check whether used), other species with a compendium and regulon truth (Bacillus subtilis with SubtiWiki regulons; other organisms in DREAM5-style resources, check usage), or a held-out part of a database not yet scored. ## 5. The PI's island idea (seeds are starting genes)

**Reading.** From a seed gene, follow strong CLR edges until the walk falls off (the set stops being cohesive); the distribution of island sizes over seeds then sets breadth, and the leak rate sets depth. This is local clustering by conductance. phi(S) = w(S, complement of S) / min(vol(S), vol(complement)); personalised-PageRank sweep cut (Andersen, Chung, Lang 2006) returns, for a seed, the prefix set of the PPR vector ordered by p_j / d_j with minimum conductance; the best-conductance-versus-size curve averaged over seeds is the network community profile (Leskovec, Lang, Dasgupta, Mahoney 2009). Closest existing methods: Markov-time stability (Delvenne, Yaliraki, Barahona 2010; Lambiotte et al.) and the map equation (Rosvall and Bergstrom 2008) as an MDL formulation. The lead's reading is right on all counts, with the corrections below.

**Is it sound?** As a diagnostic and as a depth setter, yes. As a stand-alone keff setter, no, for four reasons.
1. No strong-edge threshold is possible on the full CLR matrix. The dense positive CLR graph has background weight that dominates volume (about half of all pairs have positive z), so every conductance is near 1. Any island analysis needs a sparsified graph; the softmax top-cap graph is the natural one and carries keff. Hence island size depends on the keff used to measure it. Break the circularity either by iterating to a fixed point (keff <- measured within-island strong degree at that keff) or, better, by computing the island profile on a ladder of k-nearest-neighbour graphs (k = 5 to 80) and reading the island sizes that persist across the ladder. A weight-threshold-free alternative is to use the walk itself: the PPR vector with teleport probability alpha on the softmax graph for a ladder of alpha; each alpha gives a sweep cut. alpha is then the depth-like parameter, so island size and depth are estimated together as a 2-D profile, not one from the other.
2. MDL cannot compare different sparsifications. The map-equation code length is a code length for walks on a given graph; it can choose the partition (and with Markov-time stability the resolution) on a fixed graph, but code lengths from graphs with different k are not comparable. The map equation therefore cannot choose keff. It can choose depth (partition stable plateau across Markov time) and flag multiscale structure.
3. Island size is not the best width. The project's oracle simulation set every complex member's width to its true size n - 1: large complexes barely moved (x1.04 messy world), 20-gene complexes lost (x0.29 / x0.53), only n = 5 gained (x7.7 / x2.9). So per-gene island size as keff_i would be predicted by this simulation to help only tiny complexes. That is the mass-conservation fact (a softmax at perplexity n - 1 over unequal partners leaks mass out of the module), and it holds whatever estimator supplies the size.
4. The observation that 3 to 6 gene complexes gain nothing from diffusion is, I think, a width mismatch (keff 10 exceeds island size 3 to 6 so the walk must leak outside, then the leak is amplified for t steps), not the modularity resolution limit. The resolution limit is the opposite failure (modularity merges small modules into bigger ones); both are "scale mismatch" but the remedy differs. Markov-time stability and the NCP address the right one.

**Depth from the leak rate.** For an island S the probability of staying in S after t lazy steps is about (1 - phi(S)/2)^t, leak time about 2/phi. Mixing inside the island needs t of order 1 / gap_S (relaxation time of the within-island chain). A usable window exists only if phi(S) is much smaller than gap_S; then t* = sqrt( (1 / gap_within) * (1 / phi_out) ) (geometric mean of the window) is a natural choice, and the width of the window, log( (1 / phi_out) / (1 / gap_within) ), is a label-free module-quality diagnostic. This is the Cheeger-inequality version of the spectral rule in 2.2 and of Markov-time stability. Reachability confinement is phi near 0: the window is unbounded above and a depth cap must come from elsewhere (the second CLR's reachable background, section 3.4).

**Seeds and hubs.** Seeds are genes (graph nodes), as clarified. Use all genes if the PPR push algorithm is run per seed at tolerance epsilon (cost O(1 / (epsilon alpha)) per seed, a few milliseconds to seconds), or a stratified sample of 500 to 2,000 seeds with inverse-probability weights, strata by degree (or row SD) so hubs do not dominate the profile. Hub seeds give large islands and fall off late; peripheral genes that belong to no module give no island: the profile has a point mass of "no island" (phi_min not below a null quantile), which is the analogue of the project's partner-less genes. Define "real island" as phi_min below the 5% quantile of the same statistic on a null graph: per-gene permuted data run through the same pipeline, or a degree-preserving rewire of the same graph. Report the fraction of seeds with islands.

**The PI's seed-sampling scheme (pairs drawn log-uniformly in tail rank, endpoints as seeds, unique vertices) and the lead's cautions.**
- What it does. Equal draws per decade of tail rank puts as many draws into ranks 1 to 10 as into ranks 10^5 to 10^6, so the very strongest pairs are heavily oversampled relative to their number (duplicates are removed by the pruning, which is fine). That is deliberate and reasonable for finding the strongest islands, but it is a sample of the strongly-connected subpopulation, not of genes.
- Biases. (1) Size bias: a module with n members contributes about n(n-1)/2 strong pairs, so at a given strength the chance that a member is drawn grows with n (per gene about proportional to n); the ribosome and other large tight blocks dominate the seeds. The island-size distribution among seeds is therefore skewed to large sizes. Correct with Horvitz-Thompson weights: weight each seed by 1 / (its number of pairs in the sampled tail), or by the inverse of its degree on the sparsified graph. (2) Artefact pairs: the very top of the CLR tail is where duplicated or cross-hybridising probes, paralogs, shared guides or off-target effects, and batch-driven pairs sit; the top decades may not be biology. (3) Both endpoints of a pair lie in the same island, so taking both double-counts; take one endpoint at random. (4) How deep the tail goes: sample only down to the empirical-null gate (the rank at which the permuted-data pipeline produces about as many pairs as the real data, or lfdr under Efron central matching reaches 0.5); below that, seeds are noise and their islands are noise.
- The lead's cautions are right and I would adopt all of them. Merging near-identical islands at Jaccard above 0.5: do it greedily in order of increasing conductance (best island first, later islands assigned to it if Jaccard exceeds 0.5), not by connected components of the Jaccard graph (chaining), and also compute containment |A n B| / min(|A|, |B|): nested subcomplexes have low Jaccard but high containment and are the multi-scale signal, so keep them as separate scales and record the nesting. Report (a) the seed-weighted size distribution (what a typical seeded gene experiences, after the weights above), (b) the island-unique distribution (module sizes), and (c) coverage: the fraction of all genes belonging to at least one merged island (membership, not seeding), plus the fraction in none. Genes with no strong partner never seed; their fraction is estimated from a uniform random subsample of genes run as seeds (say 10% of genes), where the same conductance gate is applied, so the no-island fraction is not conditioned on strong seeding.
- Calibration of "falls off". Run the identical pipeline (same sampling in tail rank, same number of seeds, same merging) on per-gene permuted data, 10 to 20 permutations; define an island as real if its conductance minimum is below the 5% quantile of the permuted minima for islands of the same size and volume (conductance depends strongly on size, so match by size bins). With 500 to 2,000 seeds and many islands the per-island empirical p-values need a Benjamini-Hochberg step. Also run the degree-preserving rewire of the real graph as a second null (it separates "tight because of degree" from "tight because of modules").
- A better or additional alternative. Because PPR push costs milliseconds per seed, every gene can be a seed (10,000 seeds, minutes), which removes the sampling bias and the need for weights; then do the clustering and the gate on all islands. Keep the PI's scheme as the second, strong-edge-weighted view (seeds from the tail), since it answers a different question (what the strongest structure looks like). If sampling is kept, use stratified sampling by degree or row-SD deciles with equal seeds per stratum and Horvitz-Thompson weights, plus the uniform subsample for the no-island fraction. 500 to 2,000 seeds are sufficient for the size distribution; the no-island fraction needs the uniform sample.

**Multi-scale.** Expect multi-scale: complexes in human data are heavy-tailed in size (dimers to the ribosome), overlapping, and nested (subcomplexes). The NCP typically has no single minimum; local minima at several sizes are the scales. So a single (keff, t) is a compromise, and the two honest outputs are (a) a per-dataset scale ladder, and (b) a multi-depth mixture (average of P^t over a small set of depths, or the resolvent, a geometric mixture) which covers scales at once. A per-gene setting follows only if the per-gene island scale is stable under resampling of experiments (test: split-half agreement of each gene's phi-minimising size, with the per-gene size accepted only when it reproduces within a factor of two).

**BIC/MDL-style criterion for turning the island distribution into (keff, t).**
- Depth: on a fixed graph, choose t where the partition is persistent in Markov time (longest plateau of constant best partition in stability) or the window of the previous paragraph is widest; penalise by a null: only accept t where the island conductance is below the null quantile.
- Breadth: not from island sizes; use blocked-CV likelihood or instability (3.2). Use islands to set a lower bound for the graph's connectivity (a k below which islands cannot be formed because the graph is disconnected within complexes: fraction of seeds with within-island degree less than the planted degree) and as a diagnostic of the heavy-tail of sizes.
- A two-part MDL that is comparable across k can be built by coding the held-out data (experiments) given the graph, i.e. the held-out likelihood in 2.2, plus the cost of the graph (k G log G bits for the support); that is the only MDL that compares sparsifications and it reduces to the CV rule, since graph cost is at most a few percent of the data cost at N G of order 10^6 to 10^7 entries.

**Validation without a fresh truth.** Planted multi-scale simulation (heavy-tailed module sizes, overlap, hubs): does the NCP minimum / island-size distribution recover the planted size distribution (compare quantiles and the fraction without islands) and does the leak-window recover the planted depth? Permuted data: the island profile should coincide with the degree-preserving null. M3D and K562: the profile on each half should be stable. Do the NCP at keff ladder and check that conclusions persist.

**Cost.** PPR push for all seeds on a 10,000-node graph with 50 to 150 neighbours per node: minutes. Sweep cuts: negligible. NCP over a ladder of 8 values of k and 6 values of alpha: under an hour. Infomap on 10,000 nodes: seconds to minutes; Markov-time stability across 20 times with Louvain: minutes to an hour. Everything is cheaper than the grid.

**Verdict.** Run it, as complement to candidate 1: it tells what the graph's modular structure looks like, supplies an independent depth window, and supplies the multi-scale warning. Do not use it alone for keff.
