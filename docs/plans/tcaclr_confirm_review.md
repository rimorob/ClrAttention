# Review of the draft confirmatory preregistration `docs/plans/tcaclr_confirm_plan.md`

*Reviewer: Claude Fable 5.1 (`claude-fable-5-1`), independent statistical review, 16 November 2026. Read-only. No compute, no truth opened; the only numbers used are structure counts and stored outputs already quoted in `docs/plans/tcaclr_prereg_candidates.md`, `docs/plans/complex_confirm_design.md`, `docs/plans/complex_confirm_review.md`, `results/complex_confirm/estimate.csv`, `docs/preregistration_reciprocity_yeast.md` and `docs/STATUS.md`. The reviewer wrote `docs/plans/fable_theory_memo.md`, which the plan cites for H2; that is a conflict of interest for section 7 and is declared here.*

## Verdict

**Not ready to fix. Revisable to a defensible confirmatory test of one narrow claim, provided five things change.** As drafted, the plan (i) contradicts a standing PI declaration that no further complex-oriented operator change is tested confirmatorily, and does not say so; (ii) registers an undefined primary inference procedure under a false description of the E. coli precedent; (iii) does not disclose that Arm A has the same built-in data–truth link it discloses for Arm B; (iv) words the claim as general ("on complexes in untouched data") when the inference convention can only support a fixed-truth claim; and (v) carries a multiplicity rule for H3 that is the wrong one for the statement it gates. None of these needs new data. After the fixes, Arm A can confirm exactly this: *in HepG2 Perturb-seq pseudobulk from the same laboratory and screen family as the discovery data, tcaCLR's pooled co-membership AUPR on the Complex Portal human complexes exceeds aCLR's.* It cannot confirm transfer across technologies, generalisation to complexes not in the truth, or anything about the mechanism; Arm B can add an estimate on independent data for largely the same complexes. That narrow claim is worth having only if the PI withdraws the 1 Nov declaration on the record and declares this the last complex test of tcaCLR.

---

## 1. Can the design confirm anything? Is Arm A the right primary?

**The serial-testing problem comes first.** `docs/preregistration_reciprocity_yeast.md` line 41: "This is the third complex-oriented operator test ... It is declared the last. Whatever the verdict, no further budget- or readout-level operator change aimed at complexes is tested confirmatorily in this project." The 13 Nov review (section 0, item 2) recorded that this declaration "stays in force" and that "a3r is not adopted whatever this study shows"; the PI on 13 Nov (STATUS, "PI answers") left "whether this is the last evaluation of a3r on a new complex truth" undecided. The draft plan is a confirmatory test of a readout-level change aimed at complexes. It does not mention the declaration. A referee who reads the ledger will count the looks at tcaCLR on complex truths: K562 (selection among ~20), E. coli M3D and EcoMAC (confirmatory, failed), RPE1 (gated secondary), net4 yeast (exploratory estimation), and now HepG2 and Kemmeren. That is the pattern the 1 Nov text was written to prevent. The plan can only proceed if the PI withdraws the declaration explicitly, the withdrawal is quoted in the plan, and the plan declares itself the final complex test of tcaCLR with the E. coli failure reported in every sentence that reports this result. Without that, the design confirms nothing a referee will accept, whatever p comes out.

**Arm A as primary: defensible on power, misdescribed on independence.** Arm A has the larger n_eff (31 on the proxy universe against 10), the more novel truth content (40% of positives not in CORUM, against 10.5% novel in CYC2008), and no known reproducibility defect. Those are the right reasons to make it primary. But:

- The plan discloses for Arm B that 376 deleted genes are truth members ("co-membership can be partly built into the design") and says nothing of the kind for Arm A. The HepG2 screen is a CRISPRi essential-gene library (DepMap common-essential; `tcaclr_prereg_candidates.md` B1). Ribosomal, proteasomal and spliceosomal subunits are the core of that library. Knocking down one subunit perturbs the others, so the pseudobulk profiles contain the complex structure by design, at least as strongly as in Kemmeren. This is the same link as K562's, which is where tcaCLR was selected. It must be disclosed for Arm A in the same words, and the number of perturbed genes that are truth members must be counted (label-free) and printed with the result.
- "Untouched data" in the claim rule overstates. The HepG2 matrix has never been scored, but it is the same laboratory, library family, pipeline and (for 60% of positives) truth content as the discovery data. The claim must be worded as a same-lineage, cross-cell-type transfer. The plan's own section 2 says this correctly; the claim rule and the title do not.
- The power row rests on n_eff 31 from a proxy universe and on k transferred from yeast and the K562/RPE1 anchors, with no HepG2 experiment-variance ratio. The gate "n_eff ≥ 15" is the gene-only requirement for +0.03; with an experiment component like net4's it is 27. The gate should be the number the registered interval needs (section 2).
- Planning effect: the right anchor for a same-lab transfer is RPE1 merged CORUM, +0.036 [+0.013, +0.059] (gated secondary, descriptive), not K562's +0.059 and not net4's +0.096. Say so. At +0.03 and SE 0.0105 (gene only) the power is 0.80; at +0.03 and the total SE with ρ = 0.89, 0.56. The plan should print both and not call the arm "adequate for both" without the condition.

**Arm B: worth running as a secondary, for four minutes of compute, as an estimate.** Its value is that the data are independent of every consumed set; its weakness is the reused truth (89.5% of positives consumed), gate F, the hs1991 orientation question and the deleted-gene link. None of that is fixable, so B cannot be a confirmatory arm and should not be co-primary. Register it as a secondary estimate with the same estimand, intervals, no p-value, and the fixed sentences of the net4 design. Do not run GENIE3 or GRNBoost2 on it (7–9 h for a comparator table on a secondary arm; PIDC and |r| suffice there).

**H2 strata (post hoc from net4) are fine as a registered prediction, with two corrections.** (a) The bins 3–6, 7–19, 20+ are net4's bins, not the theory's: the theory memo's leak boundary is n < keff ≈ 10 and the large-module regime is "n well above keff"; the 7–19 bin straddles the boundary. Either disclose that the bins are empirical, or split 7–19 into 7–10 and 11–19 descriptively. (b) The memo itself cites the net4 strata (section 5.2 of the memo), so H2 is not an independent theoretical prediction; the plan's parenthesis says "derived after the net4 look", which is right, and it should add "and the theory memo that states the prediction had seen those strata".

## 2. Primary inference convention

**The plan's description is false and the procedure is undefined.** Section 5 says "Primary inference follows the *E. coli* preregistered test: resampling of genes in blocks that respect the truth's n_eff clusters, combined with resampling of experiments." The E. coli test used a paired delete-half gene jackknife on the truth's genes, no blocks, and the experiment bootstrap was a separate necessary condition, not a component of the SE (`clr_logpt_interpretation.md` section 3). n_eff is a scalar (inverse Herfindahl index of positive-pair shares); there are no "n_eff clusters" in any committed procedure. Blocks of genes that "respect" complexes are the complex-level jackknife, which the same paragraph then calls a sensitivity. As written, the primary SE cannot be computed from the text. This is a blocker.

**What the two conventions estimate.** The gene ⊕ experiment SE answers: on *these* complexes, how variable is d over which member genes and which profiles are sampled? The complex-level SE answers: how variable is d over which complexes are in the truth? On net4 the first was 0.0147 (total) and the second 0.0608, because the pooled d was carried by five complexes (CPX-1601, 1599, 1602, 2262, 1604; per-complex median about 0; 60 improved, 65 worse). That is not a disagreement between two estimates of one quantity; it is two estimands. The gene convention supports a fixed-truth claim ("on the Complex Portal human complexes"); the complex convention would support a general claim ("on protein complexes"), and the candidates memo shows no feasible truth supports the general claim (8,450 complexes needed). So the honest position is: register the fixed-truth estimand and word the claim to it, and report the complex-level interval as the reason the claim is not general.

**Is choosing the gene convention choosing the answer?** Not in origin: it was fixed on 22 Oct before any out-of-sample result, kept on 13 Nov with the series component added, and in E. coli it gave a *failure*. It becomes choosing the answer only if the claim is worded beyond what the convention estimates. The plan's title and claim rule do that. Fix the wording, not the convention.

**What I would register (Arm A).**
- Estimand: d = AUPR(tcaCLR) − AUPR(aCLR), pooled over all pairs among truth genes of the frozen Complex Portal human truth, t = 9, raw scores, `ap_fast` tie convention. *Fixed-truth estimand*, stated as such.
- Primary SE: √(SE_gene² + SE_exp²); SE_gene = SD of 500 paired delete-half gene jackknife replicates (seed fixed now); SE_exp = SD of 100 paired delete-half replicates over perturbations with all four cheap methods refitted (seeds fixed now). One-sided test at 0.025 against 0, plus the minimum-effect condition d ≥ +0.005 (which will not bind). This is the net4 convention and the only one with a precedent on this readout.
- Mandatory descriptors printed in the same row, computed by the script before anyone reads them: the complex-level delete-half interval (500 replicates); the share of d carried by the three complexes with the largest per-complex contribution; the number and fraction of complexes with ≥ 10 positives whose per-complex AP improves; and the sign-test p over those complexes (one-sided 0.025) as a registered key secondary "breadth" test. If the breadth test fails, the fixed sentence is: "The pooled gain is carried by [k] large complexes; across complexes tcaCLR did not improve the median complex." On net4 this sentence would have been written. That is the point: it is the sentence a referee will otherwise write for you.
- Power gate on the real universe: n_eff ≥ 27 (the +0.03 requirement under ρ = 0.89), or, if n_eff is between 15 and 27, the plan says before the run that only effects ≥ +0.04 are detectable and the test proceeds at that stated sensitivity. Below 15: estimate only, as drafted.
- Replicate-distribution skewness and percentile interval, as net4.

## 3. Multiplicity

- **H1.** One primary test (Arm A, one-sided 0.025). Arm B: interval and fixed sentences, no p-value. No Holm split; a co-primary split to 0.0125 halves B's already marginal power (0.40 → about 0.28 at +0.03 with ρ = 0.89) and buys a claim ("confirmed in two arms") that B's reused truth cannot support anyway. The intersection-union alternative ("both must pass") needs no α split but has joint power ≈ 0.8 × 0.4; it is not worth it.
- **H2.** No tests; three intervals per arm with pre-defined labels: "positive" = lower bound of the gene ⊕ experiment interval > 0; "near zero" = |d| < 0.01 and the interval includes 0; "not positive" = d ≤ 0.01 (the plan's tie width is fine for the small strata, SE ≈ 0.003–0.004, and should be stated against the SE). H2 is reported as "the prediction held / partly held / did not hold" by a script, never as a test. The 20+ stratum in Arm B has n_eff 4.4 and the plan correctly says it cannot be tested; say the same of its interval ("too wide to read").
- **H3.** The plan's rule is the wrong one for its statement. "tcaCLR is the best of the seven" requires all six comparisons to be won; that is an intersection-union statement, for which each test at 0.025 already controls the error at 0.025 and Holm is unnecessary and conservative. Holm over the six is needed only for *individual* statements ("tcaCLR exceeds CLR") made without requiring the others. Register both: (a) "best of seven" = all six one-sided tests at 0.025 (IUT); (b) any individual comparator statement = Holm over the six at 0.025. Add the E. coli S3 trap sentence verbatim (tcaCLR − CLR has about half the SE of tcaCLR − aCLR, k_CLR 0.016–0.027 against 0.037–0.041, so it "excludes 0" more easily). Pre-declare what happens if GENIE3 or GRNBoost2 is not approved: the family shrinks to the methods run and the statement is "best of the five run".
- **Secondaries with fixed sentences** (noribo, large-complex-only, hub-stratified, breadth test): intervals and script-chosen sentences, no p-values except the breadth sign test, which is declared a key secondary and does not gate H1.

## 4. The pooled-pair estimand and the ribosome

Keep the pooled AUPR as the primary; do not exclude or down-weight the ribosome in it. Three reasons. (i) The mechanism the project is testing predicts the gain precisely in large modules; a ribosome-excluded primary would test a different, weaker prediction and would be a post-net4 choice (the net4 noribo was +0.046 against +0.096; on E. coli the ribosome was 60% of positives). (ii) Every anchor SE and every prior result is pooled; a weighted primary would have no precedent and no k rule. (iii) Complex Portal human is less concentrated than K562 CORUM (largest share 0.075 against 0.249) while still having 67% of positives in 20+ complexes, so the pooled estimand in Arm A is already a fairer version of the K562 one.

But the primary must travel with registered key secondaries, each with its own interval and a fixed sentence: `noribo` (pre-declared ribosome list by the same rule as net4, ≥ 30% r-protein genes by a label-free regex, human equivalent RPL/RPS); `large ≥ 20` and `small 3–6`; the two subunit-rule sensitivities of net4 (`xsub_excl`, `ribo_joined`), because Complex Portal human will hold the 40S and 60S as separate complexes, making the cross-subunit pairs negatives, exactly the M2 issue of the 13 Nov review and the opposite of the K562 truth on which tcaCLR was selected; and the per-complex breadth test of section 2. The headline sentence must carry the concentration descriptor whenever the 20+ stratum accounts for more than 80% of d: "the gain is carried by complexes of 20 or more genes". That is the honest claim, it is what the theory predicts, and it is what the net4 data showed.

## 5. Controls

The comparator set (CLR, aCLR, |r|, GENIE3, PIDC, GRNBoost2) is sufficient for the "best of" statement. It is not sufficient for the paper's mechanism story, which is what a hostile referee will attack after H1 passes: "you added a log and a second standardisation; show that the walk and the log are needed and that this is not a rank trick or a hub trick." The controls below are all read-outs of the same operator and cost seconds to minutes on either arm. Every one is scored on the consumed truth by the same run, so they must be pre-declared, labelled exploratory, and never selected among.

| Control | What it tests | Expected under the mechanism | Cost (Kemmeren / HepG2) |
|---|---|---|---|
| aCLR within-row percentile (rank readout) | Is tcaCLR more than cross-row calibration? | tcaCLR > rank-aCLR > aCLR (K562: 7.95 / 5.13 / 3.51) | seconds |
| tcaCLR at t = 1 (= CLR re-standardised within candidate sets; theory memo P1) | Is the walk needed? | ≈ CLR, well below tcaCLR at t = 9 | seconds |
| CLR on raw P^9 (linear domain, a1) | Is the log needed? | below aCLR (K562 2.46×) | seconds |
| Fixed-exponent inflation of P^9, r ∈ {1.5, 2}, row z | Is the row-adaptive exponent needed? | below tcaCLR | seconds |
| Degree-preserving rewiring of A (10 draws) → walk → tcaCLR | Is the gain a normalisation artefact on hub structure? | at base rate | minutes |
| Sinkhorn-balanced A → walk → tcaCLR | The memo's speculation; hub drift removed | unknown; informative either way | minutes |
| Hub-stratified P (MI row mean) | Is the gain a hub effect? | survives with the same sign | seconds |
| Matched-depth aCLR (t = 18, 27) | Not needed here: both methods are at t = 9 and the stacking study already showed depth is not the lever | — | skip |

Register all except the last as "exploratory read-outs of the same operator, reported in one table, intervals only, none may be cited in place of H1". Compute for the whole table is under ten minutes per arm. GENIE3 and GRNBoost2 are the only expensive items (about 5 h and 3–4 h each on Kemmeren; about 5–7 h each on HepG2) and are needed only for H3; recommend running them on Arm A only, with PI approval, or on the truth-gene subset with the four cheap methods rescored on the same subset for a like-for-like H3 (about 0.5 h).

One missing *non-method* control: the deleted-gene / perturbed-gene link. Register a descriptive split of the primary universe into pairs where neither gene is perturbed in the panel versus the rest, with d in each (intervals only). If the gain is confined to pairs involving perturbed genes, the result is a property of the screen design, and the paper must say so.

## 6. Errors, overclaims and undisclosed exposure

1. **Section 5, primary inference:** false description of the E. coli procedure and an undefined "n_eff clusters" block scheme (section 2 above). Blocker.
2. **Section 0/2, the 1 Nov declaration:** not mentioned; the plan is a confirmatory complex-oriented operator test the project declared it would not run. Blocker until the PI's withdrawal is quoted.
3. **Arm A built-in link:** the essential-gene CRISPRi panel perturbs truth members; disclosed for B, not for A. Must be counted and disclosed for A.
4. **Claim rule wording:** "on complexes in untouched data" → a fixed-truth, same-lineage claim (section 2).
5. **Power:** "adequate for both" is conditional on ρ; the gate of 15 is the gene-only number; planning effect should be anchored on RPE1 (+0.036), not K562 or net4. The GENIE3 RPE1 estimate is given as 6.6 h here and 5.3 h at 40 cores in STATUS (16 Nov); reconcile.
6. **H3 multiplicity:** wrong rule for the "best of" statement (section 3).
7. **HepG2 recipe OPEN:** the pseudobulk build has many degrees of freedom (cell filters, perturbation set, normalisation, gene filter, universe). The only acceptable rule is "`analysis/perturbseq_bulk.R` as run for K562, byte-identical, with any forced deviation decided on a label-free criterion and logged before the first score". The operator universe (all measured genes, as net4) versus the truth-gene universe (as the K562 anchors) changes k; fix one and say which anchor it matches.
8. **Human truth rules OPEN (paralog rule):** copy the net4 builder verbatim, including the subunit handling and the two sensitivities; introduce no new rule for human. The drafter has seen which net4 complexes carried the effect (ribosomal subunits), so any new human-specific merge or paralog rule is exposed to that knowledge.
9. **CYC2008 licence OPEN:** if unresolved, Arm B results cannot be published; resolve (write to the Wodak lab; or cite the paper and the archive capture and state the licence status) before FIXED.
10. **Drafter exposure, incompletely stated:** the drafter has also seen the net4 per-complex table (which complexes drove d), the E. coli per-complex results, and this reviewer's theory memo, which was written with the net4 strata in view. H2's thresholds come from that exposure. Add these to the disclosure.
11. **"No tcaCLR ... value exists on any dataset named here":** true for scores; the Kemmeren expression was opened label-free (reproducibility, row names) and Complex Portal human's structure was counted on a K562 proxy universe. Say both in section 8.
12. **"Arm B ... Independent of net4":** the data are; the truth is 89.5% consumed. The sentence should read "data independent of net4; truth largely shared with the consumed Complex Portal yeast".
13. **H2 "no p-values for H2 in Arm B":** implies p-values in Arm A. State: no p-values for H2 anywhere.

## 7. P1–P8 and R1–R12: before or after, gate or diagnostic

(Reviewer's conflict: these are my own proposals.)

- **Before the real data, as label-free gates or diagnostics on the new matrices (no truth needed):** P1 (tcaCLR at t = 1 equals the within-candidate re-standardised CLR, tolerance 1e-10) as an implementation gate; P2 (row SD of log P^9 over reachable entries) and P3 (row skewness, log versus linear) as reported diagnostics with one gating threshold: if the median row SD is below 1 nat or the unreachable share exceeds 5%, the log readout is degenerate on that matrix and the arm is "not evaluable" (the net4 design's reachability gate, made explicit). Minutes.
- **Before, on spent K562 only, as mechanism support (exploratory, no gate):** P4 (column-hub correlation of Z), P5 (column-mean normaliser), P6 (one-directional versus Stouffer row-local AP). About 30 minutes. They inform the paper's theory section, not this test, and must not add readouts to the confirmatory arms beyond the exploratory table of section 5.
- **After, on the new data, as registered descriptives:** P7 (regression of the mean within-complex score on log size, slope per method) is H2 in continuous form and should replace or accompany the bins; P8 (anti-DPI triangle check) optional.
- **Simulation R1–R12:** label-free, so it may run at any time; 6–10 h at 8 threads. Run it *before* FIXED only if the plan wants H2's size boundary set from theory rather than from net4 (rule: the smallest n at which tcaCLR − aCLR > 0 in 9 of 10 seeds becomes the lower edge of the "positive" bin). Otherwise run it in parallel and report it as supporting material; it must not be used to adjust any method setting. Of the twelve, R6, R7 and R1 are the ones that matter for this plan (identity, lemma, size regime); R12 (Sinkhorn → tcaCLR) tells the plan whether its exploratory Sinkhorn control is worth the row.
