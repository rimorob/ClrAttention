## Logic review

**(i) "Stouffer combination has a standard-normal null": is the memo precise?** Yes, with one qualification. N(0,1) would require z_ij and z_ji to be independent standard normals. They are two affine images (different row mean and SD) of the same D_ij, so they cannot be independent; under within-gene permutation they correlate at 0.998 (|r|) and 0.971 (MI). Then S is about sqrt(2)·max(z, 0). If rows were exactly Gaussian that already gives P(S>3) = P(z > 2.12) = 0.0169 (my calculation), 12.5x nominal; the half-normal shape of null |r| rows (and the MI skew) takes it to 0.0377. So all three causes the memo names contribute, and the single biggest is the duplication, which does not depend on the half-normal point. The null in question is the pooled pair distribution (upper triangle) under complete independence, one permutation draw, rows standardized by their own permuted values. Even one directed z is not N(0,1) under that null (P(z>3) = 0.009 for |r|). The proposed replacement ("a ranking statistic: the two z-scores are functions of the same dependence value and are clipped at zero, so it has no closed-form null; significance, where used, is assessed by permutation") is correct. I would add "(under complete independence they correlate at about 0.998)" only if space permits. The second proposed edit (Discussion: the permutation null is conservative for CLR scores) is correct for the evidence in the memo, but see item 1: the reason differs for MI. The same sentence needs fixing in methods.md and `R/calibrate.R` (#4).

**(ii) "A pooled permutation null is very conservative" vs "S>3 in 3.8%": consistent?** Yes; they concern two different comparisons.
- The 3.8% compares the *permutation null* with the *Gaussian* reference. A user who thresholds S at a Gaussian quantile would be anti-liberal relative to the permutation null (3.8% false pairs at S>3 instead of 0.135%).
- The conservatism compares the *permutation null* with the *real data*: real S>3 occurs in 0.9% of BEELINE |r| pairs (1.8% MI) and 2.6% of K562 MI pairs, less than the null's 3.8%/3.0%/3.5%. Real positives would only add to the real tail, so the real background-null pairs are even thinner than the real distribution, and p-values taken from the permutation null are too large (conservative) for them.
- Real S>3 (0.9%) is above the Gaussian 0.135%, so neither reference is right; the empirical-null (Efron) route the memo mentions is the principled one, and its first attempt in §3.5 (signed r) is a poor implementation of it, not a test of the idea (it flags pairs less correlated than a shifted background). That is the only place where "nothing improves CLR-NS" should be read narrowly: no *tested* alternative improves it.
- The "101 of 16.1M" figure is weaker evidence than the memo suggests (#9).

**(iii) Is anything on the attention side missed or dismissed too fast?** The conclusion is mostly sound. Candidates I looked for and their status in the repo: global temperature (O1, O1t: tested in the keff test), symmetric sink/gate (S1) and profile cosine (dropped in round 2 of `background_width_exploration.md`; the memo's A9 title mentions "partner-less junk rows" but its text never says that gate and sink were closed on synthetic data, and cosine falsified), multi-head (MH, tested), Sinkhorn/column forms (asymmetry memo), unbalanced OT (reasoned only, labelled as a smooth S1), reciprocity (open, design exists). So "only reciprocity remains" agrees with the repo, but the memo should say why S1 and cosine are not on the list. What the memo does not test and does not claim: reciprocity on real K562 (disclosed), the gate on real K562 (K562 has about 10% partner-less genes; synthetic round 2 says a realizable gate costs more than it gains at f = 10%). Two soft spots:
- "The whole gain is the ribosome" rests on noRP intervals of about +-0.004 (U50 -0.0001 [-0.0040, +0.0039]); a non-RP gain below +0.004 cannot be excluded, though it would be below the project's 0.005 margin.
- The row-percentile comparison handicaps CLR by construction: CLR's pooled advantage is exactly its cross-row calibration, which a within-row rank discards, so "K10-rank above CLR-rank on all seven truths" is a comparison of two re-ranked matrices, not a rescue of the method. The memo says this correctly ("cross-row scale effect"; "not a new mechanism"). Note also it holds pooled, not per complex: for 3-9-gene complexes CLR-rank beats K10-rank in 69% of complexes (mean AP 0.0037 vs 0.0026), consistent with the memo's "small-complex deficit".
