Draft abstract and graphical abstract

For your review. Nature Machine Intelligence format (150 words, unreferenced). The comparison with learned attention (scPRINT, scGPT) is deliberately left out of the abstract for now; its result is under Human data below.

Attention normalizes each query's compatibilities within its own context, an idea older than the transformer (kernel regression, fast weights). Its first widely adopted form in computational biology, context likelihood of relatedness (CLR), remains standard for de novo, unsupervised network inference but stops at the scores. We bring back from language modelling a temperature-controlled softmax, iterated attention and depth chosen on held-out data without labels. On strong-evidence Escherichia coli regulons, attention raises pooled recovery 16% over CLR, in all 1,000 resamplings, and, controlling for hub genes, leads GENIE3 and PIDC; the gain depends on CLR's normalization. Per regulon, it ties CLR and GENIE3. The gain concentrates in large, loose regulons; on tight co-expression cliques such as ribosomal complexes, attention loses to CLR. Gains are small in human K562 cells, and on single cells attention adds nothing to rank-based CLR.

In one paragraph

CLR (2007) standardizes each gene's mutual information against that gene's own background. That is a context-normalized attention score, published a decade before transformers. It remains a standard baseline for de novo, unsupervised network inference. We complete it as an attention mechanism, with no learned parameters: a softmax over each gene's top CLR partners, then a short multi-step walk (depth t, chosen without labels on held-out experiments). On E. coli data (M3D, with the partly overlapping EcoMAC) this recovers strong-evidence transcriptional regulons better than CLR and better than GENIE3, PIDC and GRNBoost2 in pooled ranking. The gain requires context normalization, it survives controls for batch structure and, on strong-evidence regulons, for hub genes, and it concentrates in large regulatory programs. Per regulon, attention ties CLR and GENIE3. It loses on tight co-expression cliques such as ribosomal complexes. In the one independent, preregistered test (DREAM5 yeast, item 8), the co-membership gain was 5.8% (interval +1.8% to +10.0% with group-level resampling, including zero with series-level resampling), below the pre-specified 10% margin, and every method was near chance. A mechanism consistent with the E. coli results, though not isolated by them: the walk trades pair-level resolution for module-level recovery. That trade is not seen in the yeast or in silico networks (item 8).

The method in pseudocode

CLR attention has no learned parameters. Given an expression compendium X (G genes by E experiments), the operator is:

# 1. dependence between every pair of genes
D[i,j] = MI(X[i], X[j])            # B-spline MI; single cells: |r| of Blom normal scores (CLR-NS)

# 2. CLR: z-score each gene's row against its own background
for each gene i:
    z[i,j] = max(0, (D[i,j] - mean(D[i,:])) / sd(D[i,:]))
S[i,j] = (z[i,j] + z[j,i]) / sqrt(2)           # pair score; S[i,i] = 0

# 3. attention: per-gene-temperature softmax over each gene's strongest partners
for each gene i:
    C = the (at most 50) j with largest S[i,j] and S[i,j] > 0
    if |C| <= 10:  A[i,j] = 1/|C| for j in C
    else:  find temperature T_i by bisection so that
              exp( entropy( softmax(S[i,C] / T_i) ) ) = 10      # ~10 effective partners
           A[i,j] = softmax(S[i,C] / T_i)[j]
    (a gene with no candidates keeps its own mass)

# 4. lazy multi-step walk
P = 0.5 * I + 0.5 * A               # rows sum to 1: half the mass stays, half attends
M = P^t, with t = 9                  # depth chosen without labels (below)

# 5. read-out
score[i,j] = 0.5 * (M[i,j] + M[j,i])   # attention mass from i to j, symmetrised

# depth t* (label-free)
split experiments into two halves by laboratory; for each depth t:
    agreement(t) = AUPR of half-1's M_t for the top 1% of half-2's S scores
t* = geometric mean of the two directions' best t; median over 50 splits = 9

Defaults are fixed in advance and never tuned on a truth set: 10 effective partners per gene, 50 candidates at most, lazy weight 0.5, depth 9. Step 2 alone is CLR; steps 3 to 5 are what this work adds. Transformer analogy: MI is the query-key score, the CLR z-score is the scaled logit, the softmax is the softmax, and the walk is repeated attention with a residual connection.

What we have in hand

1. The headline: E. coli M3D, 466 experiments, RegulonDB regulon co-membership

  • Attention beats CLR by +0.022 AUPR [+0.019, +0.026]. Depth is re-selected without labels in each of 1,000 nested bootstrap draws, and attention is ahead in all 1,000.
  • Consistent across 50 laboratory splits (+0.022 [+0.017, +0.027]) and a replicate-aware bootstrap on the 907 chips (+0.020).
  • The held-out depth is stable: median t = 9, with the criterion flat within 1% for t = 7–11.
  • Scope: items 1 to 3 come from the development organism and lineage (M3D with RegulonDB, and the overlapping EcoMAC), after many looks (see the ledger below). The module-for-edge trade in item 4 is an E. coli pattern; it is not seen in the DREAM5 yeast or in silico networks (item 8).

2. Why it works

  • Context normalization is essential. A softmax on uncalibrated |r| or MI gets about a third of the gain and none of the coherence gain.
  • A Shapley decomposition over six components attributes the AUPR gain mainly to depth (+0.018) and CLR normalization (+0.013).

3. Controls

  • Removing 0–20 global principal components leaves the gain intact. The top components are biology, not noise: growth rate, motility (σ28) and the σS stationary-phase program.
  • Hub control: the walk converges toward well-connected genes, so every method is also scored within strata of hubness. Attention stays first on strong-evidence truths (ahead of CLR in every hub decile on M3D); on all-evidence truths it ties GENIE3. Under a stratifier that does not use the walk (row mean of raw MI; follow-up B, exploratory) attention stays ahead of CLR on M3D strong evidence (+0.18 enrichment [+0.12, +0.24]). That covers attention against CLR only, not GENIE3 or PIDC.

5. Human data

  • K562 Perturb-seq pseudobulk: every method is within 10% of random on TF truths. Attention's small DoRothEA lead over CLR survives the attention-derived hub control (it ties GENIE3 and PIDC); under the MI row-mean stratifier of follow-up B (exploratory) the lead is +0.016 enrichment [−0.010, +0.042]. It loses to CLR, GENIE3 and PIDC on protein complexes, and on knockdown-response modules per module and after hub control; 86% of the complex loss comes from ten ribosomal and pre-rRNA complexes. GRNBoost2, which does not aggregate, does about as poorly as attention on these complexes, so the loss is not specific to the walk.
  • Single cells (BEELINE: human ESC and hepatocytes, plus five mouse datasets): CLR on normal scores (CLR-NS) has the highest per-dataset mean of the methods tested, including GENIE3, PIDC and GRNBoost2, but it was chosen on these truths, it is not significantly ahead of GENIE3 or PIDC, and GENIE3 has the higher median. Attention adds nothing there.
  • Against learned attention (preregistered, run once, 2 Nov): on all seven BEELINE datasets, CLR-NS, a formula with no training, is non-inferior and superior to zero-shot attention from scPRINT, a single-cell foundation model trained for this task (Hodges-Lehmann estimate of the log2 AUPRC-ratio difference +1.03, exact interval +0.34 to +1.90, positive in 7 of 7; it holds when scPRINT gets its best readout). Three qualifications: scPRINT's attention scores the same as the same model run on permuted expression, and below a data-free gene-embedding similarity, so the comparator is weak here; the advantage is absent on cell-type-specific ChIP-seq (scPRINT higher in 11 of 14 cases, exploratory); and scGPT (two datasets, descriptive) is behind CLR-NS on attention but ahead on its token embeddings. On K562 control cells the direction is the same but smaller (+0.27, 0.09 to 0.46, descriptive). Fine-tuning each model on its own cells (descriptive) did not help: scPRINT's R0 fell slightly (−0.05 log2) and CLR-NS stayed ahead of it (+1.11, 0.41 to 1.96).

6. Perturbation targets (likely a second paper)

  • Attention-based influence ranks a perturbed TF's targets above differential expression in 12 of 15 perturbations (p = 0.015; 9 of 15, p = 0.095, under a laboratory-matched null). Suggestive, not established.
  • It is complementary to SSEM-Lasso, re-implemented from Cosgrove et al. 2008: SSEM pinpoints the regulator itself, attention its regulon.
  • Differential CLR is the natural method here and is next to port.

7. Batch effects (supplement)

  • Multi-platform EcoMAC shows platform "Christmas trees" in gene-pair plots.
  • Platform-level ComBat helps more than switching from correlation to MI.
  • One clean platform (735 arrays) beats all 2,198 pooled.

Exploratory follow-ups

Four analyses run on 11 to 13 November. None enters the paper as it stands. They are here so you can see what was tried and what it showed. Section letters A to D are used below so they are not confused with sections 1 to 8.

A. Log-scale depth re-test

Preregistered before compute (8861ae4, 11 Nov)Not a blind test

Question. Is walk depth a resolution dial, with the best depth rising from fine truths (operons) to coarse ones (regulons, sigmulons)? The first test used a linear grid (t = 1 to 32), and two best depths sat at its edge. The re-test uses a geometric grid, t = 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64, 96, 128, 192, 256, plus CLR as t = 0.

Status of the evidence. The analysis and the three possible readings were committed before any new compute. It is still not blind or confirmatory: the truths and operators were already used in the linear-grid run, and its curves had been seen. Power is small, with 3 truths in E. coli (M3D) and 5 in K562. The results document gives no interpretation beyond the pre-specified readings, and this page gives none either. The statistical review adds that the power limits are severe: reading (a) was nearly unattainable.

Pre-specified reading: (b) "not resolution-like", defined as Spearman rho between truth coarseness and log2 of the best depth being at most 0.5 in at least one organism. E. coli rho = 0.500 (n = 3); K562 rho = 0.700 (n = 5). Reading (a), rho of at least 0.9 in both organisms with no edge censoring, was not met. 0 of 8 truths were censored at the grid edge t = 256; the within-1% interval of K562 CollecTRI reaches 256. The best depth moved from the edge of the linear grid to 96 (E. coli TF regulons) and 48 (K562 CollecTRI).

What "not resolution-like" can mean here. It is the literal outcome of the pre-specified rule: the pre-specified ordering was not observed. With 3 and 5 truths and flat depth curves, the test could neither establish nor exclude it. In E. coli the rule fires unless all three truths are perfectly ordered (rho of at least 0.9 occurred in 10% of bootstrap draws, rho of at most 0.5 in 88%); the order is broken by the sigmulons (5 sets; bootstrap interval of best t 0 to 256). In K562, rho is 0.70 with CLR ranked lowest, and 0.20 over attention depths only (the preregistration records that the primary convention was chosen after the linear-grid curves were seen). The coarseness rank is nominal: the K562 KD-response modules (median 100 genes) are ranked finer than DoRothEA (median 13).

Equivalence gate, passed. At t = 0, 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32 the new run reproduces the saved linear-grid curves (AUPR, base rate, regulon coherence) to within 5.0e-16 (tolerance 1e-9; 21 E. coli rows, 35 K562 rows).

B. Robust row-standardisation of the CLR step

ExploratoryPI's ideaNot for the paper
Exploratory. Not preregistered. No variant was selected, no p-values are computed (none should be read into the intervals), K562 CORUM is not held out (it was used in the attention-width test and in earlier CORUM work), M3D RegulonDB is the project's tuning set for depth, and nothing here goes to the paper. Every variant, truth and read-out was fixed before the runs. Intervals are paired delete-half gene jackknife intervals (100 replicates) with the networks held fixed; they do not resample experiments, cells or seeds. The two tables.md files hold about 700 such intervals (711 counted); this page shows 56 of the paired contrasts. With that many, "the interval excludes 0" is not evidence of a real difference, and it is used here only to describe.

Question. CLR z-scores each gene's MI row with the row mean and SD. A gene in a module with many strong partners inflates its own row SD, which lowers the z-scores of its true partners; this could hurt large complexes such as the ribosome. Does z = (x − median) / (IQR/1.349) change the pairwise score or the attention score? Everything after the row z (truncation at 0, symmetrisation, softmax, lazy walk) is the default path, and the package itself is unchanged.

Hub-stratified contrasts: the two stratifiers disagree

C. A second CLR after the walk (a3r), and stacking it

a3r: preregistered 26 Oct, not adoptedStacking: exploratory, plan committed before compute (98f3b0d)

The problem it targets. Attention conserves mass: each gene's row sums to 1. Inside a tight complex of n proteins that are copies of one latent factor, every member therefore receives about 1/n of the factor's affinity, however strongly they co-vary. CLR has no such budget, so a ribosomal pair keeps its score regardless of complex size. That is our account of why attention loses to CLR on complexes (follow-up and diagnosis in docs/plans/corum_analysis.md).

a3r in one paragraph. Keep steps 1 to 4 of the method unchanged. In step 5, replace the raw walk mass M = Pt by its logarithm, which is the log-probability of reaching j from i in t steps (a multi-hop attention logit). Then z-score each gene's row of the log matrix against its own background, using only pairs the walk reached, and combine the two directions as in CLR (zij + zji)/√2, clipped at 0. The pipeline is CLR, softmax, walk, log, CLR: one normalization layer on each side of the attention operator. The log turns the 1/n dilution into an additive offset that the row z-score removes. No parameter is added and t = 9 is unchanged.

Pt = P^9;  L = log(Pt)  # unreachable pairs (Pt = 0) are set to missing
for each gene i:  Z[i,j] = max(0, (L[i,j] - mean_j L[i,:]) / sd_j L[i,:])   # over reachable j only
score[i,j] = (Z[i,j] + Z[j,i]) / sqrt(2)

Stacking (a3r2, a3r3, in general a3rN). a3r's output is a symmetric z-scale matrix, the same kind of object CLR hands to the softmax in step 3. So it can be fed back in: layer k + 1 is the same pipeline with the score Sk in place of the CLR score. The question was whether a second or third layer pools evidence across overlapping modules. Primary setting: t = 9 at every layer. Controls in the same run: a single layer at deeper t = 18 and 27 (the same total reach), a plain repeat of the CLR step on a3r's output, a shorter second layer (t = 2, 4), and hub-stratified scores.

What the preregistered a3r test showed (26 October)

  • Complex superiority over attention at t = 9 was required at one-sided p < 0.025 on both E. coli datasets. It was +0.026 AUPR on M3D (p = 0.079) and +0.029 on EcoMAC (p = 0.051), so it did not confirm. The jackknife standard error on complexes was 0.018, and the smallest effect the design could detect was about 0.05.
  • Regulon non-inferiority (margin 0.005 AUPR) passed on both: +0.004 and +0.003.
  • Verdict: not adopted. Attention stays the method. The K562 numbers in the table (7.95 against CLR 6.39) are selected, not fitted: a3r has no fitted parameters, so it cannot memorise the truth, but it was picked as the best of about 20 read-outs by its K562 scores, and the best of many noisy scores is biased upward. The 20 candidates were highly correlated and the gain is large, so the bias is probably a fraction of the gap, but it has not been measured. The effect points the same way on RPE1 and E. coli, neither confirmatory. On RPE1, another cell line and a descriptive look only, a3r is +0.036 [+0.013, +0.059] AUPR above attention on merged CORUM.

What stacking showed (exploratory)

  • Neither two nor three layers meets the rule written before the run, on K562 or on E. coli, with either hub stratifier. The rule required a K562 CORUM gain of at least +0.005 over a3r and over the matched-depth control, with intervals excluding 0, and no loss over 0.005 on DoRothEA or CollecTRI.
  • K562: a3r2 is +0.0031 [−0.0027, +0.0089] over a3r on CORUM and +0.0025 [−0.0036, +0.0087] over a single layer at t = 18. It loses on CollecTRI (−0.006), knockdown response (−0.044) and CORUM without ribosomal complexes (−0.003). Three layers lose more (CollecTRI −0.011, knockdown response −0.079).
  • E. coli: a3r2 is non-inferior on strong-evidence regulons (+0.0006) but not on all-evidence regulons (−0.0055, lower bound −0.009). Three layers fail both (−0.006 and −0.012). Complex AUPR rises (+0.036 for two layers, +0.047 for three), but the interval for two layers includes 0, a single deeper layer gives only +0.001, and hub stratification roughly halves the gain. The truth is small.
  • A shorter second layer is much worse (t = 2: 1.63 on K562 CORUM; t = 4: 3.10). A plain repeat of the CLR step on a3r's output is not idempotent and is worse than a3r (5.85 and 6.83 for the two variants of the repeat).
  • What happens to the operator: nothing collapses, but the share of pairs the walk has not reached grows from 0.28% (one layer) to 5.7%, 12.3% and 17.2% (K562, layers 2 to 4), and the stationary distribution drifts away from layer 1's (Spearman 0.74, 0.52, 0.40).
Summary. A single layer already carries the K562 complex gain over attention. A second layer adds a gain that is small, inside the noise and no larger than the same reach from a single deeper layer, and it costs the regulon and knockdown truths. Read at the point estimates alone, a3r2 leads on K562 CORUM (8.19) and strong-evidence regulons (1.73) and the a3r family beats attention and CLR on both. The a3r2-over-a3r gaps (+0.003, +0.0006) are inside the noise, though, and a3r2 gives up all-evidence regulons, CollecTRI and knockdown response, so a3r, the simpler form, is the candidate. It has not been confirmed (K562 and M3D are both tuning sets for it), and stacking it is not a lever. The depths tried were t = 9 at every layer, with a second layer at t = 2, 4 and 9 only. Layers longer than 9 were not run. Full tables: docs/explore_stack_results.md.

D. a3r on yeast protein complexes: an exploratory estimate

Plan fixed before compute (design rev 2, 099c26d)Exploratory estimation, not a confirmatory testRun once

Question. How large is the pooled AUPR difference between a3r and attention on protein complexes, in a dataset and truth where a3r had never been run? The 26 October test on E. coli could not confirm (+0.026 and +0.029, p = 0.079 and 0.051) and was underpowered. A new confirmatory test was designed first. It is an estimate in a second organism, not a confirmation, and a3r is not adopted.

Many looks. The first six rows are confirmatory tests with a fixed design, and one of them (scPRINT) was positive; the seventh is an estimation study, not a test. The E. coli headline of items 1 to 4 is not one of them; it comes from development on M3D. Around the six sit dozens of exploratory analyses on the same M3D/RegulonDB and K562 truths (FDR operators, value transforms, multiscale algorithms, width rounds, rCLR, robust standardisation, macro averaging, hub controls, granularity). The nested bootstrap protects the depth choice. The other settings (10 effective partners, 50 candidates, lazy weight 0.5, the MI estimator, the truth construction with operon exclusion) were settled during development on the same data. Only the yeast tests lie outside that lineage: the section 8 test, and the a3r yeast estimate (follow-up D), which is exploratory and used the same expression matrix as section 8.

What did not work

  • Hard FDR-thresholded operators: they isolate 62–77% of genes.
  • Value transforms and diffused-profile readouts.
  • Wider gene contexts on single cells.
  • Data-driven choice of attention width: all three options failed review before running, and were tested empirically anyway under a preregistration (finished 27 October). The headline width (10 effective partners) is unchanged and no variant meets the decision rule. A separate exploratory width study, on synthetic and label-free data with no truth opened, closed negatively on 31 October.
  • A label-free multiscale algorithm based on spectral plateaus: there are no plateaus; the structure is a continuum.
  • Yeast reciprocity test: not run. Its preregistered data-quality gate failed in all 10 draws (median chance-corrected reproducibility 0.12 for mutants against 0.30 for wild types), so by the preregistration no truth was opened. Reciprocity stays a synthetic-only candidate (Supplementary Note 18).
  • CLR applied to log Pt as a fix for complexes: preregistered, failed to confirm (26 October; complex superiority p = 0.079 on M3D and 0.051 on EcoMAC; regulon non-inferiority passed). Attention stays the method.
  • Stacking a3r (a second and third attention layer on the log-then-CLR score): exploratory, 12 November. Neither two nor three layers meets the rule written beforehand on K562 or E. coli (follow-up C); the K562 complex gain over one layer is +0.003 [−0.003, +0.009] and a single deeper layer matches it.
  • Depth as a discrete "resolution dial" ordering operons → regulons → sigmulons: not supported on a linear depth grid, and the preregistered re-test on a geometric grid to t = 256 did not find the fine-to-coarse ordering either (pre-specified reading (b); with 3 and 5 truths it could not have established or excluded it, follow-up A). Across truths, the best depth is not clearly related to median module size (slope 0.20; module-bootstrap interval 0.08 to 0.67, which ignores variation between truths; across the 7 truths −0.77 to 1.16; the sign depends on the operon truth). Within regulons, an earlier per-set analysis on the linear grid found best depth rising with set size (t ∝ n0.43) but not for random sets.

  • GENIE3, GRNBoost2 and PIDC on the BEELINE single-cell benchmark: done. CLR is comparable to GENIE3 and PIDC. GRNBoost2 (legacy early stopping) is below CLR in 6 of 7 datasets (mean log2 −0.15; Wilcoxon P = 0.031, sign P = 0.125; hESC is the exception), a descriptive count, not a significant difference.

Decisions for you and me

  1. Venue: Nature Machine Intelligence (150-word abstract) or Nature Communications.
  2. Scope: one paper on CLR attention, with perturbation targets and differential CLR as a second paper.
  3. The central claim: "better recovery of regulatory programs from expression alone", not "better network inference". Is that strong enough for the target venue? The statistical review suggests scoping the claim to E. coli, or presenting the work as a reframing of CLR with characterised trade-offs, since every positive result comes from the development organism and lineage.
  4. The human single-cell story: CLR-NS rather than attention is the single-cell result. Does it belong in this paper?
  5. The yeast result: Y-M has a positive estimate under the 10% margin (the verdict "Equivalent" is on the boundary with Inconclusive), Y-E is inconclusive, and the network 3 pattern (module gain, edge cost) is not independent of the headline. How should this enter the paper and the abstract, which is currently scoped to E. coli?
  6. What to do with a3r. The evidence now is: selected on K562 (in-sample); not confirmed on E. coli (+0.026, +0.029, underpowered); an exploratory estimate of +0.096 on yeast, carried by large complexes, with an interval that includes 0 when complexes are the unit; behind CLR and GENIE3 on direct edges in E. coli. Is the headline method attention, a3r, or a presentation of both with their trade-offs? The decision of 12 November (not adopted) stands until you change it.
  7. Whether the yeast estimate is the last evaluation of a3r on a new complex truth for the paper. Not decided. The yeast Complex Portal truth is now consumed.
