## Method overview

**The method ladder: six steps, each changing one thing.**
The ladder starts from CLR (step 1) and adds one change at a time: attention with a per-gene temperature and a 9-step walk (aCLR, step 2, touching per-gene temperature and walk depth), a second CLR on the walk matrix (tcaCLR, step 3, second CLR), a walk depth derived from the data (tcaCLR2, step 4, depth rule), one temperature for all genes (step 5, temperature) and an average over 13 temperatures (step 6, temperature scale). Steps 1 to 4 are cumulative and fixed in advance; steps 5 and 6 are exploratory variants (not preregistered, tested only on truths that had already been used). The small matrices at the right show the same 11 genes of the E. coli M3D compendium (4297 genes, 466 profiles) read through each step: lexA, its five highest CLR partners, three genes ranked by the walk and two randomly drawn background genes. Each matrix is scaled to its own maximum with the diagonal left blank, so the panels show pattern only; no step is ranked against another here.

## CLR

**CLR: each pair of genes is scored against the background of each gene.**
The input is a genes-by-profiles expression matrix (here M3D, 4297 genes by 466 profiles, with 11 genes displayed). (1) A B-spline estimator gives the mutual information (MI) of every gene pair. (2) For each gene (row), the MI values to all other genes define that gene's own background, summarised by a mean and standard deviation; for lexA the mean is .081 and the standard deviation .050, and the partner dinI at MI .485 sits 8.1 standard deviations above it. (3) Negative z-scores are set to 0, which here affects 35 of the 110 off-diagonal cells shown. (4) The two directions are combined into the symmetric score S_ij = (max(z_ij, 0) + max(z_ji, 0)) / sqrt(2); for lexA and dinI this is (8.1 + 10.1) / sqrt(2) = 12.9, while lexA and the background gene yeaR score 0 because both z-scores are negative. The 2007 paper describes the Euclidean combination of the two z-scores; the 2007 software offered both Euclidean and Stouffer, and the Stouffer form is the one drawn. Nothing is learned in this step and no truth or label is used.

## aCLR

**aCLR: CLR scores become attention with a temperature set for each gene, followed by a lazy walk.**
The input is the CLR score matrix of the previous figure (M3D shown; query gene lexA). (1) Each gene keeps its 50 highest positive scores (cap 50). (2) A softmax over those scores turns them into weights, with the temperature tau solved separately for each gene so that the gene attends to about 10 effective partners (the exponential of the entropy of its weights equals 10); for lexA tau is 1.62. (3) The weights form a row-stochastic attention matrix A. (4) A lazy walk P = 0.5 I + 0.5 A is run for t = 9 steps and read out symmetrically as (P^9 + P^9') / 2. The lower panels show real rows: genes at the 10th, 50th and 90th percentile of tau over all 4297 genes (rlmL 0.31, flgJ 0.68, napF 2.25) and lexA each reach exactly 10.0 effective partners, because the temperature follows the spread of the gene's own scores (all genes have 50 positive candidates; tau ranges from 0.085 to 9.7). The fixed parameters are 10 effective partners, cap 50, lazy weight 0.5 and depth 9; the temperatures are computed from the data, not tuned, and nothing is read from any truth.

## tcaCLR

**tcaCLR: a second CLR on the logarithm of the walk matrix.**
The input is the walk matrix P^9 of aCLR (lazy walk, 10 effective partners, cap 50, lazy weight 0.5, t = 9; M3D, same 11 genes). (1) Entry (i, j) of P^9 is the chance that a 9-step walk from i is at j, shown times 100. (2) For each gene, the logarithm of its row over the genes the walk reaches (lexA reaches 4293 of the 4296 others) defines a background with a mean and standard deviation (-13.6 and 2.3 for lexA), and each entry becomes a z-score; unreached pairs stay out of the background and score 0. (3) Negative z-scores are set to 0. (4) The two directions are combined as in CLR, S2_ij = (max(z_ij, 0) + max(z_ji, 0)) / sqrt(2); for lexA and dinI this is (4.7 + 4.5) / sqrt(2) = 6.5. The walk spreads weight across the whole module of the example, so the genes shown score within a narrow band of each other, while the two background genes remain low. The step is a normalisation of the walk matrix; it adds no parameter, no learned weight and no use of any truth.

## tcaCLR2

**tcaCLR2: the walk depth t2 is derived from permutations of the data, with no truth read.**
The input is the expression matrix only. Each gene's profile is permuted on its own, 10 times; the CLR scores and the aCLR operator (10 effective partners, cap 50, lazy weight 0.5) are rebuilt on every permuted matrix; the largest non-trivial eigenvalue of the symmetrised lazy operator is taken for each (the grey spectra), and the edge is their mean (0.886 for M3D). The depth is t2 = max(1, round(ln 0.05 / ln edge)), the 0.05 convention being that the noise component of the walk has decayed to 5% of its initial size; for M3D this gives t* = 24.73 and t2 = 25. The five datasets give M3D 25 (edge 0.886), EcoMAC 18 (0.846), K562 29 (0.903), HepG2 32 (0.910) and Kemmeren 23 (0.876). The observed spectrum (blue) is drawn for comparison only and does not enter the rule. Only the depth is adapted: keff (10), the cap (50) and the lazy weight (0.5) are not, and no regulon, complex or other truth, and no held-out score, is read to set t2.

## Global temperature

**Global temperature: one tau for all genes in place of one per gene (exploratory variant).**
This variant is exploratory: it was not preregistered and was tested only on truths that had already been used. It keeps the CLR scores, the top-50 candidates, the lazy walk (t = 9) and the second CLR, and replaces only the temperature of the softmax: a single tau is solved so that the mean number of effective partners over all genes is 10 (0.5412 for M3D; 0.6188 for EcoMAC, taken from the stored table). On the same real rows, the per-gene rule gives rlmL, flgJ, lexA and napF exactly 10.0 effective partners each, while the global tau gives them 28.7, 6.7, 1.4 and 1.0. Over all 4297 M3D genes the global rule yields effective partners from 1.0 to 46.4 (10th, 50th and 90th percentile 1.0, 5.4 and 26.8) with mean 10.0, whereas the per-gene temperatures themselves span 0.085 to 9.7. The figure is descriptive: it shows how the two rules distribute attention and makes no comparison of network quality.

## Multi-scale

**Multi-scale: the average of each pair's rank percentile over 13 temperatures (exploratory variant).**
This variant is exploratory: it was not preregistered and was tested only on truths that had already been used. At each of 13 global temperatures (log2 tau from -4 to +2 in half octaves, mean effective partners 1.2 to 44.6 on M3D) the same walk (t = 9, as drawn) and second CLR give one score per pair. MS1 is the mean over the 13 temperatures of each pair's rank percentile among all 9.2 million pairs; MS2 is the mean of the pair's score standardised within each temperature (z over all pairs); MS3 is MS1 over the 8 temperatures from log2 tau -2.5 to +1. All temperatures are weighted equally and none is chosen, tuned or dropped. In the example, pairs inside the group of lexA, dinI and their partners are at the top percentile at every temperature (MS1 = 1.00), whereas pairs with the background genes move with the temperature (for example lexA-rpoN 0.35, lexA-yeaR 0.75).

## Comparison methods

**Comparison methods: what each one scores.**
GENIE3 fits, for each target gene, a random-forest-type tree ensemble that predicts the target's expression from all other genes; a candidate regulator's score for that target is its importance in the ensemble, giving one directed score per ordered pair. GRNBoost2 follows the same per-target scheme with gradient-boosted trees and early stopping. PIDC discretises expression, computes the mutual information of each pair and decomposes it by partial information decomposition against every third gene; a pair's score aggregates the share of its information that is unique to the pair, and is undirected. The fourth method is the absolute Pearson correlation of the two genes' profiles. The tree and information diagrams are drawings without data; the scatter shows the real M3D profiles of lexA and recA (r = 0.83 over 466 profiles). The panel states what each method computes and carries no statement about results.
